## What Paragard Breakage Has Done to Houston Women

The injuries that result from Paragard breakage during removal include:

- **Uterine perforation** — tears or punctures in the uterine wall that may require surgical repair
- **Embedded fragments** — broken pieces of the copper arm or polyethylene core lodged in the uterus, requiring hysteroscopic or surgical retrieval
- **Migration of broken fragments** — pieces moving through the uterine wall to the abdominal cavity, bladder, or pelvic organs
- **Multiple surgical procedures** — hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, laparotomy, sometimes hysterectomy
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)** — secondary infection from retained fragments
- **Chronic pelvic pain** — long-term pain from scarring or retained material
- **Adhesions / scar tissue** — affecting uterus, fallopian tubes, ovaries
- **Loss of fertility** — primary infertility (cannot become pregnant) or secondary (cannot maintain pregnancy)
- **Hysterectomy** — permanent removal of the uterus due to severe damage

For Houston women treated at the Texas Medical Center facilities, documentation of these injuries tends to be unusually thorough because TMC providers maintain detailed electronic medical records, perform comprehensive imaging (ultrasound, CT, MRI), and produce detailed operative reports. Houston Paragard claimants often have stronger medical record documentation than claimants in regions with less robust healthcare infrastructure.

### Honest Context: The First Bellwether Defense Verdict

Houston women considering filing Paragard claims need to know about the first bellwether outcome and what it means:

The first bellwether trial concluded in January 2026 with a jury verdict for Teva Pharmaceuticals on the failure-to-warn claim. This was a setback for plaintiffs and has caused some legal commentators to lower settlement value projections for the litigation. However, **this does not mean Paragard cases are no longer viable**:

- Bellwether trials test specific plaintiffs with specific facts; one defense verdict does not establish that all plaintiffs lose
- Subsequent bellwether trials with different plaintiffs may produce different outcomes
- Mass tort litigation typically does not reach global settlement after a single bellwether — multiple data points are needed
- Plaintiff legal strategy adapts after early trials, often improving subsequent presentations
- Settlement may still occur at lower values than initially projected

The honest read for Houston Paragard claimants: cases are now harder to value than they were in 2024-2025, but they are not worthless. We are open with claimants about this context and will help you make an informed decision about whether filing is right for your situation.
