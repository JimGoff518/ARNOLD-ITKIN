## What Paragard Breakage Has Done to Texas Women

The injuries that result from Paragard breakage during removal include:

- **Uterine perforation** — tears or punctures in the uterine wall
- **Embedded fragments** — broken pieces of the copper arm or polyethylene core lodged in the uterus
- **Migration of fragments** — pieces moving through the uterine wall to other organs
- **Multiple surgical procedures** — hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, laparotomy, hysterectomy
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)**
- **Chronic pelvic pain**
- **Adhesions and scar tissue**
- **Loss of fertility** — primary or secondary infertility
- **Hysterectomy**

For Texas military and veteran claimants specifically, the medical record completeness through DoD and VA systems often provides unusually strong documentation. For rural Texas claimants, medical record completeness depends on the specific healthcare provider — but your attorney has subpoena power and other tools to recover records you cannot obtain on your own.

## Texas-Specific Statute of Limitations Considerations

Texas's two-year personal injury statute runs from the date of injury OR from the date the injured person knew or should have known the injury was linked to a defective product (the "discovery rule").

For Paragard claims, the discovery rule is particularly important because many women whose IUDs broke during removal initially attributed the injury to provider error or normal procedural risk, not to a product defect. The connection between Paragard breakage and product-defect liability became more widely understood as MDL 2974 was filed and reported on starting in 2020-2021.

Practical implications:

- **Women injured 2022 or later** are generally within the standard two-year window
- **Older injuries** may still qualify under the discovery rule
- **The defense verdict in the first bellwether does NOT affect statute of limitations analysis** — it affects valuation, not viability

Do not assume your claim is time-barred without speaking with an attorney.
