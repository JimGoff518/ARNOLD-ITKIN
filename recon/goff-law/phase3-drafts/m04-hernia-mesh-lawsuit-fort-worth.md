## What Is Hernia Mesh and Why Is It Dangerous?

A hernia is a tear or weak spot in the muscle wall that lets an organ or tissue push through. Surgeons fix it by closing the gap. Starting in the 1990s, U.S. hernia surgeons began using synthetic patches — "mesh" — to reinforce the repair and reduce the chance the hernia would come back. Mesh is now used in about 90% of hernia repairs in the United States.

The problem is the way most of those meshes got onto the market. They were cleared through the FDA's 510(k) process, which only requires the manufacturer to show the new product is "substantially equivalent" to something already approved. Full clinical safety trials were not required. The manufacturers used that loophole to push polypropylene meshes — many with coatings or design features that had never been tested in long-term human studies — into surgeons' hands.

The failures in real patients have been severe and well documented:

- **Polypropylene degradation.** The plastic breaks down inside the body, releasing fragments and triggering chronic inflammation.
- **Mesh contraction.** Some meshes shrink by as much as half their original size after implantation, pulling on tissue and entrapping nerves.
- **Migration.** Mesh moves out of position, sometimes eroding into the bowel, bladder, or other organs.
- **Adhesions.** Mesh adheres to organs it should not touch, causing bowel obstructions that can require emergency surgery.
- **Chronic infection.** Some designs harbor bacteria in biofilms that antibiotics cannot reach, requiring explant.
- **Inflammatory coating reactions.** Specific products — like Atrium's C-QUR with its fish-oil coating — have been linked to severe foreign-body reactions described by some surgeons during explant as "green slime."

The most notorious failure was the **Ethicon Physiomesh**, which Johnson & Johnson voluntarily recalled worldwide in 2016 after data showed unacceptably high rates of recurrence and revision surgery. That recall is now central evidence in the Ethicon MDL.

**You were implanted with a covered product.** The federal MDLs cover specific product lines:

- **Ethicon (Johnson & Johnson)** — Physiomesh (recalled 2016), Proceed, Prolene, Ultrapro

- **Covidien / Medtronic** — Parietex, Parietene, ProGrip, Symbotex, Permacol

Other manufacturers (Gore, Boston Scientific, Teleflex) are also litigated in some venues.

**You experienced a documented complication.** The most common qualifying complications are:

- Chronic pain lasting three months or more
- Mesh infection
- Mesh migration or erosion into organs
- Bowel obstruction or adhesions
- Hernia recurrence requiring repeat repair
- Revision surgery (partial mesh removal)
- Explant surgery (complete mesh removal)
- Fistula formation
- Nerve damage

**You filed within the statute of limitations** — see the section below on the Texas two-year rule and how the discovery rule extends it.

If you had hernia surgery in Fort Worth or Tarrant County in roughly the last decade and have struggled with complications since, you may have a claim. Family members of patients who have died from mesh-related complications may file wrongful death and survival claims.

## The MDL Process: What Happens When You File

Hernia mesh litigation is unusual because it is not one MDL — it is several, each tied to a specific manufacturer:

- **MDL 2846** — Bard / Davol — U.S. District Court, **Southern District of Ohio**, Judge Edmund A. Sargus Jr.
- **MDL 2327** — Ethicon — U.S. District Court, **Northern District of Georgia**, Judge Richard W. Story
- **MDL 2753** — Atrium — U.S. District Court, **District of New Hampshire**, Judge Landya B. McCafferty
- **MDL 2782** — Covidien / Medtronic — U.S. District Court, **District of New Hampshire**, Judge Landya B. McCafferty

A Multidistrict Litigation is not a class action. In a class action, everyone gets lumped together and splits a common pool. In an MDL, each plaintiff keeps an individual case with individual damages. The court coordinates pretrial work — discovery, expert witnesses, manufacturer document production — so the same fights do not get re-fought thousands of times. The court then tries a small group of "bellwether" cases that set the framework for settlement.

Multiple hernia mesh MDLs have already produced bellwether plaintiff verdicts. Several manufacturers are in active settlement negotiations. New cases continue to be filed because patients keep discovering mesh complications years after surgery.

You do not travel to Ohio, Georgia, or New Hampshire. You do not give a deposition unless your case is picked as a bellwether. The case is built from medical records, operative reports, pathology, imaging, and expert opinions.

## Texas Statute of Limitations for Hernia Mesh Cases

Texas has a two-year statute of limitations on personal injury claims under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. That sounds tight, but for most hernia mesh cases the **discovery rule** changes when the clock starts.

Mesh complications are typically "latent" — they do not appear at the time of surgery. They show up months or years later. Your hernia repair might have been in 2016 or 2018, but the chronic pain, bowel obstruction, or infection that eventually traced back to the mesh might not have surfaced until 2023 or later. Under the discovery rule, the two-year clock starts on the date you knew — or reasonably should have known — that the mesh was the cause of the injury, not the date it was implanted.

For many patients, that means the clock starts at the explant or revision surgery, when a surgeon removes the mesh and pathology confirms the damage. For others, it starts when an imaging study or specialist visit first identifies the mesh as the source.

Translation: do not assume your case is time-barred because your surgery was years ago. Talk to a lawyer first. For more detail, see our [Statute of Limitations for Texas Personal Injury Claims](/faq/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-texas/) page.

## What Compensation Could Cover

A successful hernia mesh claim can recover:

- **Past medical bills** — revision and explant surgery, hospitalization, imaging, pain management, follow-up care
- **Future medical costs** — ongoing pain management, future surgeries, monitoring
- **Lost wages** — income missed during treatment and recovery
- **Lost earning capacity** — when permanent injury affects your ability to work
- **Pain and suffering** — chronic pain, lost quality of life, the emotional toll of repeated surgery
- **Loss of consortium** — for spouses, the loss of intimacy and household contribution
- **Wrongful death** — for families of patients who have died from mesh complications

Hernia mesh cases are **products liability** cases — not medical malpractice. Texas's medical-malpractice damage caps do not apply. Compensation is driven by your actual losses, the strength of the documentation in your file, and the manufacturer's settlement posture in the MDL.
