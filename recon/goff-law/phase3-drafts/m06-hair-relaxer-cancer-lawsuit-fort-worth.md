## What Are Chemical Hair Relaxers and Why Are They Dangerous?

Chemical hair relaxers are products used to permanently straighten tightly coiled hair. They contain strong alkaline agents (lye or no-lye formulas) plus a category of chemicals known as **endocrine disruptors** — substances that interfere with how the body's hormones signal and regulate cell growth. The chemicals most often named in the federal litigation include:

- **Phthalates** (DEHP, DBP) — plasticizers used in fragrance carriers
- **Parabens** — preservatives with documented hormonal effects
- **Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives**
- **Cyclosiloxanes**
- **Bisphenols**, including BPA

These compounds are absorbed through the scalp, especially when the scalp is irritated, burned, or scabbed from a relaxer treatment — which is common. Repeated absorption over years and decades is what the science now connects to hormone-driven cancers.

### The NIH/NIEHS Sister Study

In October 2022, researchers at the **National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences** published findings from the **Sister Study** — a long-running NIH prospective study of nearly 34,000 women. The lead author, Dr. Alexandra White, reported that women who used chemical hair relaxers **more than four times per year had more than double the risk of uterine cancer** compared to women who never used them. The Sister Study became the scientific foundation for the federal lawsuits filed in the months that followed.

### Brands Named in the Litigation

The federal MDL targets the manufacturers, not the stores or salons. The most commonly named products include:

- **Dark and Lovely** (L'Oréal / SoftSheen-Carson)
- **Optimum** (L'Oréal / SoftSheen-Carson)
- **Soft Sheen** (L'Oréal)
- **Just for Me** (Strength of Nature)
- **African Pride** (Strength of Nature)
- **Olive Oil** brands (Namaste Laboratories)
- Other relaxer products manufactured by **Dabur USA** and **Godrej**

## Who Qualifies for a Hair Relaxer Lawsuit in Fort Worth?

You may qualify to file a federal hair relaxer claim if all of the following apply:

1. **History of chemical hair relaxer use** — typically for several years, with multiple applications per year. The NIH study used "more than four times per year" as the elevated-risk threshold.
2. **A qualifying cancer diagnosis** — uterine cancer, endometrial adenocarcinoma, ovarian cancer. Uterine fibroids and endometriosis are sometimes secondary claim types.
3. **Medical records confirming the diagnosis** — pathology reports, imaging, surgical records.
4. **Filing within the Texas statute of limitations** (or under the discovery rule — explained below).

You do **not** need to remember every brand you used. Family members, longtime friends, beauty supply receipts, salon records, and prior medical intake forms often help reconstruct use history. Your attorney can help with that.

Family members of a woman who died from a qualifying cancer may file wrongful-death and survival claims on her behalf.

## The MDL Process — What Happens When You File

Federal hair relaxer cases are consolidated in **MDL 3060** before Judge Mary M. Rowland in the **U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois**. A multidistrict litigation ("MDL") is not a class action. Each woman keeps her own individual lawsuit. The cases are grouped together only for the early stages — discovery, expert witnesses, and a small set of **bellwether trials** that act as test cases.

Here is what filing actually looks like for a Fort Worth claimant:

1. **Free intake call.** You tell us your use history and your diagnosis. We tell you honestly whether you appear to qualify.
2. **Sign a contingency agreement.** Zero out-of-pocket cost. We only get paid if you recover.
3. **Records collection.** We pull your medical records, pathology, and (where available) salon and pharmacy records.
4. **Filing in the MDL.** Your individual case is filed and assigned an MDL case number.
5. **Bellwether trials and global settlement.** As the early test cases produce data, the manufacturers and plaintiffs' steering committee negotiate. Most MDLs end in a structured global settlement rather than a trial for every plaintiff.
6. **You stay in Fort Worth.** You do not travel to Illinois unless your specific case is selected as a bellwether (extremely rare). Most depositions can be done remotely or here in DFW.

## Texas Statute of Limitations

Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code **§ 16.003** sets a **two-year statute of limitations** for personal injury and product liability claims. For hair relaxer cases, that clock can be measured in two ways:

- **Date of diagnosis** — two years from the date you were diagnosed with the qualifying cancer; or
- **Discovery rule** — two years from the date you knew or should have known that your cancer was linked to chemical hair relaxer use.

Because the NIH Sister Study was not published until **October 2022**, many older diagnoses still qualify under the discovery rule. But the discovery rule is fact-specific and the defense fights it hard. The safer course is to file as soon as you know you have a claim. Do not assume you are time-barred without a free consultation.

## What Compensation Could Cover

Hair relaxer claims are **products liability** claims against chemical manufacturers. They are not medical malpractice. Texas's medical malpractice damages caps do not apply. Categories of recovery in a federal products case can include:

- Past and future medical expenses (surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, ongoing oncology follow-up)
- Lost wages and lost future earning capacity
- Pain and suffering, mental anguish, and disfigurement
- Loss of fertility or hysterectomy-related damages
- Loss of consortium for the spouse
- Wrongful death and survival damages where the woman has died

We do not advertise specific settlement amounts. The MDL is active, bellwether trials are ongoing, and value depends heavily on the type of cancer, stage at diagnosis, age, duration of use, and documentation. We will give you an honest assessment in your free consultation.
