## What Is the Amazon DSP Model and Why Does It Matter?

When an Amazon-branded van pulls onto your block, the driver is usually **not** technically an Amazon employee. Amazon runs a program called the **Delivery Service Partner (DSP)** program. A DSP is a small contracting company that hires the drivers, leases the Amazon-branded vans, and operates out of an Amazon delivery station. The drivers wear Amazon uniforms, drive Amazon-branded vans, deliver Amazon packages, and follow routes generated by Amazon's algorithm — but on paper, they work for a DSP, not for Amazon.

This is a **liability shield**. When there is a crash, Amazon's first move is to say: "We don't employ that driver. Talk to the DSP." The DSP often turns out to be undercapitalized, carries only the minimum required insurance, and may even dissolve after a serious accident.

The reality on the road is different from the paperwork. Amazon controls almost everything that matters:

- **The routes** — generated by Amazon's algorithm, not the DSP
- **The delivery windows** — Amazon dictates the time pressure
- **The package counts** — Amazon assigns 200 to 300+ stops per driver per day
- **The technology** — Amazon's apps, GPS, and in-vehicle cameras monitor every minute
- **The scorecard** — Amazon's performance metrics decide whether a DSP keeps its contract
- **The vans** — Amazon-branded and leased through Amazon-affiliated programs
- **The uniforms** — Amazon-mandated
- **The customer relationship** — every interaction the driver has with you is as "Amazon"

### Why Courts Are Piercing the DSP Shield

Courts across the country have increasingly allowed plaintiffs to pursue Amazon directly on several legal theories:

- **Actual agency / respondeat superior** — Amazon exercises so much operational control that the DSP and its drivers are functioning as Amazon's agents. Courts in California, Georgia, New York, and Texas have allowed these claims past summary judgment.
- **Apparent agency** — Amazon's branding, uniforms, and customer-facing communications create a reasonable belief that the driver works for Amazon. Survey research indicates that more than 70% of consumers believe Amazon delivery drivers are Amazon employees.
- **Joint employment** — both Amazon and the DSP function as employers of the driver.
- **Non-delegable duty** — safe operation of commercial vehicles on public roads is a duty Amazon cannot contract away.
- **Direct negligence** — Amazon's time pressure, unrealistic delivery quotas, and inadequate training are themselves a cause of the wreck.
- **Negligent entrustment** — Amazon provides the van and the route to a driver Amazon knew or should have known was overworked or unsafe.

This is why an experienced lawyer matters. The named driver and the DSP are not the only defendants. **Amazon is.**

## Who Qualifies for an Amazon Delivery Vehicle Lawsuit in Fort Worth?

You may have a case if all of the following apply:

1. **You were involved in a crash in the Fort Worth area** (or anywhere in Texas).
2. **The other vehicle was an Amazon-branded delivery van or truck**, OR an Amazon Flex driver in a personal vehicle delivering Amazon packages, OR a driver in an Amazon uniform.
3. **You were injured** — even minor injuries should be evaluated, but the strongest cases involve hospitalization, surgery, ongoing treatment, lost work, or permanent impairment.
4. **You filed (or are filing) a police report** — the CR-3 Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report is critical.
5. **You can document the Amazon connection** — photos of the van, dash-cam footage, witness statements, or the police report identifying the vehicle.

Family members of someone killed in an Amazon delivery vehicle crash may file wrongful-death and survival claims.

## The Lawsuit Process — Individual Suit, Not a Class Action

An Amazon delivery vehicle accident is **not** a multidistrict litigation or class action. It is an individual personal injury / wrongful death lawsuit filed in Texas state court (or federal court if diversity jurisdiction applies). The litigation usually involves multiple defendants:

- **The driver** (often judgment-proof on his or her own)
- **The DSP** (the contracting company that hired the driver — usually under-resourced)
- **Amazon.com, Inc. and Amazon Logistics, Inc.** (the deep-pocket defendants)
- **Insurance carriers** for the DSP, Amazon, and Amazon's excess umbrella

Here is what filing actually looks like:

1. **Free intake call** with our DFW office.
2. **Evidence preservation letter** to Amazon and the DSP within 24 to 72 hours. Amazon's vans carry **Netradyne AI cameras** that record forward-facing and driver-facing footage. Amazon's logistics apps log GPS, route, and delivery times. If a preservation letter does not go out fast, that data gets overwritten.
3. **Police report and crash-scene investigation.** We pull the CR-3, photos, 911 audio, and any nearby surveillance.
4. **Identifying the DSP.** The Amazon van's plate, the route number, and the delivery station identify the contracting DSP.
5. **Demand and negotiation, then suit.** We file against the driver, the DSP, Amazon, and the carriers.
6. **Discovery and depositions.** We depose the driver, the DSP owner, and Amazon corporate representatives. We force production of scorecard data, route algorithm output, and camera footage.
7. **Settlement or trial.**

## Texas Statute of Limitations

Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code **§ 16.003** gives you **two years** from the date of the crash to file a personal injury or wrongful death lawsuit. Unlike a products-liability case, the **discovery rule** is rarely available for vehicle crashes — you knew you were hit. The two-year clock is hard.

**Do not wait.** Surveillance footage from nearby businesses gets overwritten in 7 to 30 days. Amazon's Netradyne camera data is on a retention schedule Amazon controls. Witnesses forget. The window to preserve evidence is short, and it starts the day of the crash.

## What Compensation Could Cover

Amazon delivery vehicle cases are **commercial vehicle negligence** and **corporate liability** claims. They are not medical malpractice, and Texas's medical malpractice damages caps do not apply. Categories of recovery can include:

- Past and future medical expenses
- Lost wages and lost future earning capacity
- Pain and suffering, mental anguish, and disfigurement
- Loss of consortium for the spouse
- Wrongful death and survival damages for the family of someone killed
- Property damage to your vehicle
- In rare cases involving egregious conduct, **punitive (exemplary) damages**
