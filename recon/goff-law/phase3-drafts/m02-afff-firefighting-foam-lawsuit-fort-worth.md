## What Is AFFF and Why Is It Dangerous?

AFFF stands for "Aqueous Film-Forming Foam." The U.S. Navy and 3M co-developed it in the 1960s as a way to put out jet-fuel fires that water cannot extinguish. The foam contains PFAS — per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — long-chain fluorinated chemicals that smother fuel and form a vapor-suppressing film over burning liquid.

The same chemistry that makes the foam work makes it nearly impossible for your body to eliminate. PFAS are called "forever chemicals" because the carbon-fluorine bond is one of the strongest in nature. When you absorb PFAS through your skin, breathe them in as vapor, or pick them up off contaminated gear, they enter your bloodstream and stay there. They accumulate in your kidneys, liver, thyroid, and other organs over years of exposure.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) classifies PFOA and PFOS — the two main AFFF compounds — as drinking-water contaminants with no safe level. The C8 Science Panel, an independent scientific body that grew out of an earlier DuPont settlement, published findings in 2012 establishing "probable links" between PFAS exposure and kidney cancer, testicular cancer, thyroid disease, ulcerative colitis, and other conditions. Internal manufacturer documents pulled into court through discovery show 3M and DuPont knew PFAS were toxic by the 1970s. They kept selling the foam. They never warned the firefighters using it.

## Who Qualifies for an AFFF Lawsuit in Fort Worth?

Three things have to line up.

**Documented exposure.** You worked with or around AFFF foam. For Fort Worth claimants, that almost always means one or more of these populations: Fort Worth Fire Department personnel, U.S. Navy and Marine Corps firefighters at Naval Air Station Fort Worth Joint Reserve Base, U.S. Air Force firefighters from the Carswell AFB era, Texas Air National Guard 136th Airlift Wing personnel, Alliance Airport ARFF crews, Meacham International Airport firefighters, civilian contractors who worked the burn pits, and mutual-aid responders from surrounding Tarrant County departments. Service records, training logs, employment files, and DD-214s can all document exposure.

**A qualifying diagnosis.** The federal AFFF MDL recognizes several PFAS-linked cancers and disorders. The most common qualifying diagnoses are:

- Kidney cancer
- Testicular cancer
- Pancreatic cancer
- Thyroid cancer (and certain thyroid diseases)
- Liver cancer
- Ulcerative colitis

**A medical link.** A treating or evaluating physician will provide a written "nexus opinion" tying your diagnosis to your PFAS exposure history. Your attorney coordinates this evaluation. You do not pay for it out of pocket.

If you served at any of the Fort Worth AFFF sites between the late 1960s and the late 2010s and were diagnosed with one of these conditions, you may have a claim. Family members of deceased firefighters or veterans whose deaths were linked to a qualifying condition may also file a wrongful death claim.

## Fort Worth–Specific AFFF Exposure Sites

Fort Worth's AFFF footprint is heavier than almost any city its size in the country. That is mostly because of the military aviation history layered over the western half of the city.

**Naval Air Station Fort Worth Joint Reserve Base (NAS JRB).** This is the big one. NAS JRB has been an active jet-fuel base for decades — Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force Reserve, and Texas Air National Guard units have all operated out of it. AFFF was the primary firefighting agent for every flight-line emergency and every live-burn training drill from the 1960s onward. NAS JRB and the surrounding neighborhoods (White Settlement, Westworth Village, parts of west Fort Worth) have well-documented PFAS contamination. The Department of Defense has formally identified NAS JRB as a PFAS investigation site.

**Carswell Air Force Base legacy.** Before NAS JRB existed in its current form, the site was Carswell Air Force Base, home to B-36, B-52, and KC-135 bomber and tanker operations from the late 1940s through the 1990s. Every one of those decades involved AFFF use on the flight line. If you served at Carswell as an Air Force firefighter or crew chief, your exposure went back to some of the earliest AFFF formulations — the ones with the highest PFOS and PFOA concentrations.

**Alliance Airport.** Alliance is a major industrial and cargo airport in north Fort Worth/Roanoke. Its ARFF crew has used Class B foam since the airport opened in the late 1980s. FedEx, Amazon, and other major cargo operators run heavy aircraft through Alliance, which means regular ARFF training with AFFF.

**Meacham International Airport.** Fort Worth's older general aviation airport maintained ARFF capability with AFFF for decades.

**Fort Worth Fire Department.** Fort Worth Fire began using Class B foam in the 1970s for refinery responses, vehicle fires, and industrial calls. The FWFD training academy used AFFF in live-burn exercises for many years. Engine companies that responded to fuel-tanker wrecks on I-20, I-30, I-35W, or the 820 loop often deployed foam at the scene.

**Surrounding municipal departments.** Arlington, Grand Prairie, Bedford, Euless, Hurst, Haltom City, North Richland Hills, Saginaw, Burleson, Mansfield, and the rest of the Tarrant County mutual-aid network maintained Class B foam capability for industrial and highway incidents.

**Industrial sites.** The refineries, fuel-storage facilities, and chemical plants ringing Fort Worth — and the industrial fire brigades that protected them — kept AFFF stocked for years.

If you trained or fought fires at any of these sites, you were exposed.

## The MDL Process: What Happens When You File

Every federal AFFF personal injury lawsuit in the country has been consolidated into MDL 2873 before Judge Richard M. Gergel in the District of South Carolina. An MDL — a Multidistrict Litigation — is not a class action. In a class action, everyone gets lumped together and split a single pool of money. In an MDL, each plaintiff keeps an individual case with individual damages. The court coordinates pretrial work — discovery, expert witnesses, manufacturer document production — so the same fights do not happen thousands of times over.

The MDL then selects a small set of representative "bellwether" cases for trial. Those trials test how juries weigh the science and set the range that the manufacturers and plaintiffs use to negotiate global settlement frameworks. After bellwethers, most cases settle individually — but each case is evaluated on its own facts: cancer type, length and intensity of exposure, age at diagnosis, lost income, and medical costs.

You do not travel to South Carolina. You do not give a deposition unless your case is selected as a bellwether. Almost all of the case is built from documents, medical records, and expert opinions. Most Fort Worth AFFF cases will resolve through settlement, not trial.

## Texas Statute of Limitations for AFFF Cases

Texas has a two-year statute of limitations on personal injury claims under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. That seems short. For most AFFF firefighter cases, it is not the problem people expect — because the **discovery rule** changes when the clock starts.

Under the discovery rule, the two-year clock does not start when you were exposed. It starts when you knew — or reasonably should have known — that your injury was linked to AFFF. For most firefighter cancer claims, that means the clock started when you (or your doctor) connected your diagnosis to your PFAS exposure history. For many veterans and firefighters, that connection was not even publicly knowable until 2016 or later, when the link between AFFF and specific cancers entered mainstream medical and legal awareness.

In practical terms, many older diagnoses — going back into the 1990s and 2000s — still qualify under the discovery rule. Do not assume you are too late without talking to a lawyer first. ## What Compensation Could Cover

A successful AFFF claim can recover:

- **Past medical bills** — chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, hospitalization, imaging, follow-up care
- **Future medical costs** — ongoing oncology care, monitoring, possible recurrence treatment
- **Lost wages** — income missed during treatment and recovery
- **Lost earning capacity** — when the cancer or its treatment permanently affects your ability to work
- **Pain and suffering** — the physical and emotional toll of diagnosis and treatment
- **Loss of consortium** — for spouses, the loss of companionship and household contribution
- **Wrongful death** — for families of firefighters and veterans who have died from a qualifying cancer

AFFF cases are **products liability** cases. Texas's medical-malpractice damage caps do not apply. Recovery is determined by your actual losses, the strength of the science in your specific file, and the defendants' willingness to settle versus go to trial.
