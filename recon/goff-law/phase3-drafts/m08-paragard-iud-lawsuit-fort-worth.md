## What Is Paragard and Why Is It Dangerous?

Paragard is the only **non-hormonal** intrauterine device approved by the FDA in the United States. It is a small, T-shaped polyethylene frame wrapped in copper wire, first approved in **1984** and continuously marketed since. The copper itself is the contraceptive — it creates an environment inside the uterus that is hostile to sperm. The device is designed to sit in the uterus for up to ten years and to be removed in a routine in-office procedure that takes minutes.

The device was originally manufactured by FEI Products, sold to Duramed Pharmaceuticals, acquired by **Teva Pharmaceuticals USA** in 2008, and then sold to **CooperSurgical** (a subsidiary of The Cooper Companies) in 2017. Both Teva and CooperSurgical are now primary defendants in MDL 2974.

### The Breakage Problem

The central allegation in the federal lawsuits is that Paragard's plastic-and-copper "arms" can become brittle while the device sits in the uterus over the years. When the OB-GYN pulls on the retrieval string at the end of the device's lifespan, the arm can **fracture** — leaving a sharp fragment embedded in the uterine wall or, in worse cases, migrating through the wall into the abdomen or other pelvic organs.

The injuries that follow include:

- **Uterine perforation** — a tear or puncture in the uterine wall
- **Embedded copper fragments** lodged in the uterus
- **Migration** — fragments traveling into the abdominal cavity, bladder, or bowel
- **Surgical retrieval** — hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, or open laparotomy
- **Hysterectomy** in serious cases
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)** from retained foreign material
- **Adhesions and scar tissue** affecting the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries
- **Chronic pelvic pain**
- **Infertility** — primary or secondary
- **Repeated surgeries** to retrieve all fragments

The legal theories in MDL 2974 include **design defect, manufacturing defect, failure to warn, breach of warranty, and negligent misrepresentation**.

## Who Qualifies for a Paragard Lawsuit in Fort Worth?

You may qualify to file in MDL 2974 if all of the following apply:

1. **The device was a Paragard copper IUD** — not Mirena, Kyleena, Liletta, or Skyla. Those are hormonal IUDs by different manufacturers and have separate litigation.
2. **The device broke, fractured, fragmented, or perforated the uterus** during insertion or (more commonly) during removal.
3. **You sustained documented injury** — perforation, embedded fragments, surgical removal, hysterectomy, PID, chronic pain, infertility, or related complications.
4. **The injury is in your medical records** — operative reports, ultrasound or X-ray confirming the fragment, pathology from any retrieval surgery.
5. **You file within the Texas statute of limitations** (see below).

Family members of a woman who died from Paragard complications may file wrongful-death and survival claims.

You do **not** need to have kept the broken IUD fragments. The medical records do the work.

## The MDL Process — What Happens When You File

Federal Paragard cases are consolidated in **MDL 2974** before **Judge Leigh Martin May** in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia. An MDL is not a class action. Each woman keeps her own case — they are grouped only for early-stage discovery, expert work, and a small set of **bellwether trials** that serve as test cases.

Here is what filing actually looks like for a Fort Worth claimant:

1. **Free intake call.** You describe the device, the removal event, and your injuries. We tell you honestly whether you appear to qualify.
2. **Sign a contingency agreement.** Zero out-of-pocket cost.
3. **Records collection.** We pull operative reports, imaging, pathology, and OB-GYN records from Texas Health, JPS, Methodist, Baylor Scott & White, or wherever you were treated.
4. **Filing.** Your individual case is filed in MDL 2974 and assigned an MDL case number.
5. **Bellwether trials and settlement.** As bellwether trials produce data, the parties negotiate. Most MDLs end in a structured global settlement rather than a trial for each plaintiff.
6. **You stay in Fort Worth.** You do not travel to Georgia unless your specific case is selected as a bellwether (very rare). Most depositions can be conducted remotely or in DFW.

The MDL is active. New cases continue to be filed. Filing now keeps you in the cohort if a global settlement is structured later.

## Texas Statute of Limitations

Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code **§ 16.003** sets a **two-year statute of limitations** for personal injury and product liability claims. For Paragard cases, the clock typically runs from one of two dates:

- **Date of injury** — usually the date the device broke during removal, or the date the perforation or retained fragment was diagnosed; or
- **Discovery rule** — the date you knew or should have known the injury was linked to a defective product.

The discovery rule is fact-specific and the defense fights it. The safer course is to file as soon as you know you have a claim. Do not assume you are time-barred without a free consultation.

## What Compensation Could Cover

A Paragard claim is a **products liability** claim against the manufacturer. It is **not** medical malpractice — your OB-GYN is not a defendant — and Texas's medical malpractice damages caps do not apply. Categories of recovery in a federal products case can include:

- Past and future medical expenses (surgeries, anesthesia, follow-up imaging, fertility treatment, hysterectomy, hormone replacement therapy)
- Lost wages and lost future earning capacity
- Pain and suffering, mental anguish, and disfigurement
- Loss of fertility damages
- Loss of consortium for the spouse
- Wrongful death and survival damages where the woman has died
