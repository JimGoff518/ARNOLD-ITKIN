## What Are Chemical Hair Relaxers and Why Are They Dangerous?

Chemical hair relaxers are products applied to the hair to straighten its natural texture. They have been a generational beauty staple, especially in Black communities, with many women beginning use as young children and continuing for decades. Brands include **Dark & Lovely**, **Optimum**, **Soft Sheen-Carson**, **Just for Me**, **Olive Oil** products, **African Pride**, and many others — most manufactured or distributed by **L'Oréal USA**, **SoftSheen-Carson** (a L'Oréal subsidiary), **Strength of Nature**, **Namaste Laboratories**, and **Dabur USA**.

The chemistry that straightens hair also delivers a steady, repeated dose of endocrine-disrupting compounds — chemicals that interfere with the body's normal hormone signaling. Independent testing and the federal litigation identify several categories of concern:

- **Phthalates** — plasticizers (often DEHP and DBP) used in fragrance formulations; classified as endocrine disruptors
- **Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives** — used to prevent product spoilage; formaldehyde is a known human carcinogen
- **Parabens** — preservatives with documented hormone-disrupting properties
- **Cyclosiloxanes** — silicone additives that contribute to product texture
- **Bisphenols** — including BPA, well-known for endocrine disruption

These compounds are absorbed through the scalp, especially through the small burns and scalp irritation that frequent relaxer use commonly produces. The science now links cumulative exposure to **uterine cancer**, **endometrial cancer**, **ovarian cancer**, **uterine fibroids**, and **endometriosis** — all hormone-driven conditions.

The October 2022 study — formally titled "Use of Straighteners and Other Hair Products and Incident Uterine Cancer," published in the *Journal of the National Cancer Institute* and led by Dr. Alexandra White of the NIH's National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences (NIEHS) — analyzed data from the Sister Study, a long-running prospective cohort of nearly 34,000 U.S. women. The finding that frequent users had more than twice the uterine cancer risk of non-users is the scientific anchor of the federal MDL.

## Who Qualifies for a Hair Relaxer Lawsuit in Dallas?

You may qualify if you can show four things.

**Use.** You used chemical hair relaxers regularly, typically over a period of years. Self-application or salon application both count. Use does not have to be limited to a single brand — most women used multiple brands across their lifetime.

**A qualifying diagnosis.** The primary qualifying conditions in MDL 3060 are:

- Uterine cancer (including endometrial adenocarcinoma)
- Endometrial cancer
- Ovarian cancer

Secondary claim types include uterine fibroids and endometriosis. Frequency and duration of use matter — the NIH Sister Study identified use of more than **four times per year** as significantly elevating uterine cancer risk.

**A medical link.** A treating or evaluating physician provides a written "nexus opinion" linking your specific diagnosis to your chemical hair relaxer use. Your attorney coordinates this evaluation. You do not pay for it out of pocket.

**Timely filing.** Your claim must be filed within the Texas statute of limitations — though the discovery rule almost always applies for hair relaxer cases (see below).

If you used relaxers regularly during your life and have been diagnosed with one of the qualifying cancers, you may have a claim. Family members of women who have died from a qualifying cancer may file wrongful death and survival action claims.

## The MDL Process: What Happens When You File

All federal hair relaxer cancer cases have been consolidated into **MDL 3060** before Judge Mary M. Rowland in the **Northern District of Illinois (Chicago)**. The MDL was established after the October 2022 NIH study triggered a wave of filings. A Special Settlement Master, Ellen K. Reisman, was appointed in 2025 to begin coordinating global settlement discussions.

A Multidistrict Litigation is not a class action. In a class action, everyone gets lumped into a single settlement. In an MDL, each plaintiff keeps an individual case with individual damages. The court coordinates pretrial work — discovery, expert testimony, manufacturer document production, bellwether trials — so the same procedural fights do not happen thousands of times. After bellwethers, most cases resolve through individual settlements valued on the strength of each plaintiff's own facts: cancer type and stage, age at diagnosis, length and intensity of relaxer use, documentation strength, and the specific manufacturer defendants involved.

You do not travel to Illinois. You do not give a deposition unless your case is selected as a bellwether. The case is built from medical records, oncology pathology, brand-use history (including family corroboration), and expert opinions.

## Texas Statute of Limitations for Hair Relaxer Cases

Texas has a two-year statute of limitations on personal injury claims under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. That sounds short. For hair relaxer cases, the **discovery rule** almost always extends it.

The link between chemical hair relaxers and uterine cancer was not even publicly known until the NIH study published in October 2022. A woman diagnosed with uterine cancer in 2017 could not have known her hair relaxer use was the cause until five years after the diagnosis. Under the Texas discovery rule, her two-year clock starts when she knew — or reasonably should have known — that the cancer was linked to relaxers, not on the date of diagnosis.

In practical terms, that means most women diagnosed with a qualifying cancer in roughly the last decade still have time to file, and many older diagnoses still qualify under the discovery rule. Do not assume you are too late. Talk to a lawyer first. ## What Compensation Could Cover

A successful hair relaxer claim can recover:

- **Past medical bills** — surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, hospitalization, imaging, follow-up oncology care
- **Future medical costs** — ongoing oncology monitoring, recurrence treatment, fertility-related care if applicable
- **Lost wages** — income missed during treatment and recovery
- **Lost earning capacity** — when cancer or its treatment permanently affects your ability to work
- **Pain and suffering** — the physical and emotional toll of cancer diagnosis and treatment
- **Loss of fertility** — for women whose treatment included hysterectomy or oophorectomy
- **Loss of consortium** — for spouses
- **Wrongful death** — for families of women who have died from a qualifying cancer

Hair relaxer cases are **products liability** cases. Texas's medical-malpractice damage caps do not apply. Compensation is determined by your actual losses, the strength of the documentation in your file, and the defendants' settlement posture.
