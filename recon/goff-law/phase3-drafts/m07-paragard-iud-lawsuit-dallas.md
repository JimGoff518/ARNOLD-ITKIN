## What Is Paragard and Why Is It Dangerous?

Paragard is a small, T-shaped intrauterine device wrapped in copper wire. The FDA first approved it in **1984** as the only non-hormonal long-acting reversible contraceptive on the U.S. market. It is designed to sit in the uterus for up to ten years and to be removed in a routine in-office procedure that takes a few minutes.

The device was originally manufactured by FEI Products, sold to Duramed Pharmaceuticals, acquired by **Teva Pharmaceuticals USA** in 2008, and most recently sold to **CooperSurgical** (a subsidiary of The Cooper Companies) in 2017. Both Teva and CooperSurgical are now primary defendants in MDL 2974.

### The Breakage Problem

The central allegation in the lawsuits is that Paragard's plastic-and-copper "arms" can become brittle while the device sits in the uterus over the years. When the OB-GYN attempts a routine removal by pulling on the string, the arm can **fracture** — leaving a fragment embedded in the uterine wall or, in worse cases, migrating through the wall into the abdomen or pelvic organs.

The injuries that follow include:

- **Uterine perforation** — a puncture or tear in the uterine wall
- **Embedded fragments** — broken copper pieces lodged in the uterus
- **Migration** — fragments traveling into the abdominal cavity, bladder, or other organs
- **Surgical retrieval** — hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, or open laparotomy
- **Hysterectomy** — in serious cases
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)** — from retained foreign material
- **Adhesions and scarring** affecting the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries
- **Chronic pelvic pain**
- **Infertility** — primary or secondary
- **Repeat surgeries** to retrieve all fragments

### What the Manufacturers Allegedly Knew

The plaintiffs in MDL 2974 allege that Teva and the predecessor manufacturers received reports of Paragard breakage events for years but did not update the warning label or the Patient Information sheet to disclose the risk. Discovery has surfaced internal documents the plaintiffs allege show the manufacturers blamed breakage on "user error" by the removing physician rather than on the design of the device.

The legal theories in the MDL include **design defect, manufacturing defect, failure to warn, breach of warranty, and negligent misrepresentation**.

## Who Qualifies for a Paragard Lawsuit in Dallas?

You may qualify to file in MDL 2974 if all of the following apply:

1. **The device was a Paragard copper IUD** — not Mirena, Kyleena, Liletta, or Skyla. Those are hormonal IUDs made by different manufacturers and are subject to separate litigation.
2. **The device broke, fractured, fragmented, or perforated the uterus** during insertion or (more commonly) during removal.
3. **You sustained documented injury** — perforation, embedded fragments, surgical removal, hysterectomy, PID, chronic pain, infertility, or related complications.
4. **The injury appears in your medical records** — operative reports, ultrasound or X-ray confirming the fragment, pathology from any retrieval surgery.
5. **You file within the Texas statute of limitations** (see below).

Family members of a woman whose death resulted from Paragard complications may file wrongful-death and survival claims.

You do **not** need to have saved the broken IUD pieces. The medical records do the work.

## The MDL Process — What Happens When You File

Federal Paragard cases are consolidated in **MDL 2974** before **Judge Leigh Martin May** in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia. An MDL is not a class action. Each woman keeps her own case — they are grouped only for discovery, expert work, and a small set of **bellwether trials** that act as test cases.

Here is what filing looks like for a Dallas claimant:

1. **Free intake call.** You describe the device, the removal event, and your injuries. We tell you honestly whether you appear to qualify.
2. **Sign a contingency agreement.** Zero out-of-pocket cost. We are only paid if you recover.
3. **Records collection.** We pull operative reports, imaging, pathology, and OB-GYN records.
4. **Filing.** Your individual case is filed in MDL 2974 and assigned an MDL case number.
5. **Bellwether trials and settlement.** As bellwether trials produce data, the parties negotiate. Most MDLs end in a structured global settlement rather than a trial for every plaintiff.
6. **You stay in Dallas.** You do not travel to Georgia unless your specific case is selected as a bellwether (very rare). Most depositions can be conducted remotely or in DFW.

The MDL is **active**. New cases continue to be filed. Bellwether trials have already occurred and continue to be scheduled. Filing now keeps you in the cohort if a global settlement is structured later.

## Texas Statute of Limitations

Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code **§ 16.003** sets a **two-year statute of limitations** for personal injury and product liability claims. For Paragard cases, the clock typically runs from one of two dates:

- **Date of injury** — usually the date the device broke during removal, or the date the perforation or retained fragment was diagnosed; or
- **Discovery rule** — the date you knew or should have known the injury was linked to a defective product.

The discovery rule is fact-specific and the defense fights it. The safe play is to file as soon as you understand you have a claim. Do not assume you are time-barred. Call for a free review and let an attorney evaluate the dates.

## What Compensation Could Cover

A Paragard claim is a **products liability** claim against the manufacturer. It is **not** medical malpractice — your OB-GYN is not a defendant — and Texas's medical malpractice damages caps do not apply. Categories of recovery in a federal products case can include:

- Past and future medical expenses (surgeries, anesthesia, follow-up imaging, fertility treatment, hysterectomy, hormone replacement therapy)
- Lost wages and lost future earning capacity
- Pain and suffering, mental anguish, and disfigurement
- Loss of fertility damages where applicable
- Loss of consortium for the spouse
- Wrongful death and survival damages where the woman has died
