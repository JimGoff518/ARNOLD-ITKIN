## What Is Hernia Mesh and Why Is It Dangerous?

A hernia happens when an organ or tissue pushes through a weak spot in the muscle wall that is supposed to hold it in. Surgeons repair it by sewing the gap shut. Starting in the 1990s, almost every U.S. hernia surgeon began using a synthetic patch — "mesh" — to reinforce the repair. The mesh is supposed to stay put, integrate with your tissue, and prevent the hernia from coming back.

The problem is that many of the meshes that flooded the market were never tested the way drugs are tested. Most got onto the market through the FDA's 510(k) clearance process, which only requires the manufacturer to show the new product is "substantially equivalent" to something already approved. Full clinical safety trials were not required. When the products failed in real patients, the failures were not minor.

Documented problems include:

- **Polypropylene degradation.** The plastic used in many meshes breaks down inside the body, releasing fragments and causing chronic inflammation.
- **Mesh contraction.** Some meshes shrink by as much as half their original size after implantation, pulling on tissue and nerves.
- **Migration.** Mesh moves from where the surgeon placed it, sometimes eroding into the bowel, bladder, or other organs.
- **Adhesions.** Mesh sticks to organs it should not touch, causing bowel obstructions.
- **Chronic infection.** Some mesh designs harbor bacteria in biofilms that antibiotics cannot reach, requiring explant surgery.
- **Inflammatory coating reactions.** Specific products — like Atrium's C-QUR with its fish-oil coating — have been linked to severe foreign-body reactions.

The most notorious failure was the **Ethicon Physiomesh**, which Johnson & Johnson voluntarily recalled worldwide in 2016 after data showed unacceptably high rates of recurrence and revision surgery. The recall is now central evidence in the Ethicon MDL.

**You were implanted with a covered product.** The major MDLs cover specific manufacturer product lines:

- **Ethicon (Johnson & Johnson)** — Physiomesh (recalled 2016), Proceed, Prolene, Ultrapro
- **Atrium** — C-QUR, C-QUR Edge, C-QUR V-Patch, C-QUR Tacshield
- **Covidien / Medtronic** — Parietex, Parietene, ProGrip, Symbotex, Permacol

Other manufacturers (Gore, Boston Scientific, Teleflex) are also litigated in some venues.

**You experienced a documented complication.** Common qualifying complications are:

- Chronic pain lasting three months or more
- Mesh infection
- Mesh migration or erosion into organs
- Bowel obstruction or adhesions
- Hernia recurrence requiring repeat repair
- Revision surgery (partial mesh removal)
- Explant surgery (complete mesh removal)
- Fistula formation
- Nerve damage

**You filed within the statute of limitations** — see the section below on Texas's two-year rule and how the discovery rule extends it.

If you had a hernia repair in Dallas in roughly the last decade and have struggled with complications, you may have a claim. Family members of patients who have died from mesh-related complications may file wrongful death and survival claims.

## The MDL Process: What Happens When You File

Hernia mesh litigation is unusual because it is not one MDL — it is several, each tied to a specific manufacturer:

- **MDL 2846** — Bard / Davol — U.S. District Court, **Southern District of Ohio**, Judge Edmund A. Sargus Jr.
- **MDL 2327** — Ethicon — U.S. District Court, **Northern District of Georgia**, Judge Richard W. Story
- **MDL 2753** — Atrium — U.S. District Court, **District of New Hampshire**, Judge Landya B. McCafferty
- **MDL 2782** — Covidien / Medtronic — U.S. District Court, **District of New Hampshire**, Judge Landya B. McCafferty

A Multidistrict Litigation (MDL) is not a class action. In a class action, everyone is lumped together. In an MDL, each plaintiff keeps an individual case. The court coordinates pretrial work — discovery, expert witnesses, manufacturer document production — so the same fights do not happen thousands of times over. The MDL then tries a small group of representative "bellwether" cases. Those verdicts set the framework for global settlement.

Multiple hernia mesh MDLs have already had bellwether trials and significant plaintiff verdicts. Several manufacturers have entered active settlement negotiations. New cases continue to be filed because patients keep discovering mesh complications years after surgery.

You do not have to travel to Ohio, Georgia, or New Hampshire. You do not give a deposition unless your case is selected as a bellwether. The case is built from medical records, operative reports, pathology, imaging, and expert opinions.

## Texas Statute of Limitations for Hernia Mesh Cases

Texas has a two-year statute of limitations on personal injury claims under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. On its face, that sounds like a tight window. For most hernia mesh cases, the **discovery rule** changes that.

Mesh complications are often "latent" — they do not show up at surgery, they show up months or years later. Your hernia repair might have happened in 2017, but the chronic pain or bowel obstruction or infection that eventually traced back to the mesh may not have shown up until 2023. Under the discovery rule, the two-year clock starts on the date you knew — or reasonably should have known — that the mesh was the cause of your injury, not the date of implantation.

For many patients, that means the clock starts at the explant or revision surgery, when a surgeon physically removes the mesh and pathology confirms the damage. For others, it starts when an imaging study or specialist consultation first connects the complication back to the mesh.

Translation: do not assume your case is time-barred because your surgery was years ago. Talk to a lawyer first. For a fuller explanation, see our [Statute of Limitations for Texas Personal Injury Claims](/faq/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-texas/) page.

## What Compensation Could Cover

A successful hernia mesh claim can recover:

- **Past medical bills** — revision and explant surgery, hospitalization, imaging, pain management, follow-up care
- **Future medical costs** — ongoing pain management, future surgeries, monitoring
- **Lost wages** — income missed during treatment and recovery
- **Lost earning capacity** — when permanent injury affects your ability to work
- **Pain and suffering** — chronic pain, lost quality of life, the emotional toll of repeated surgery
- **Loss of consortium** — for spouses, the loss of intimacy and household contribution
- **Wrongful death** — for families of patients who have died from mesh complications

Hernia mesh cases are **products liability** cases. Texas's medical-malpractice damage caps do not apply. Compensation is determined by your actual losses, the strength of the documentation in your file, and the specific manufacturer's settlement posture in the MDL.
