## What Is AFFF and Why Is It Dangerous?

AFFF stands for "Aqueous Film-Forming Foam." The U.S. Navy and 3M developed it in the 1960s to put out jet-fuel fires that water cannot touch. The foam works because it contains long-chain fluorinated chemicals — known as PFAS, or per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — that smother the fuel and starve the fire of oxygen.

The problem is the same chemistry that makes the foam effective also makes it nearly impossible for your body to get rid of. PFAS are called "forever chemicals" for a reason. The carbon-fluorine bond is one of the strongest in nature. When PFAS enter your bloodstream — through your skin, your lungs, or your gear — they build up in your kidneys, liver, thyroid, and other organs. They stay there for years. They pass to children through pregnancy and breastfeeding.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) has formally classified the two most common AFFF compounds — PFOA and PFOS — as contaminants with no safe level in drinking water. The C8 Science Panel, an independent scientific review created from earlier DuPont litigation, published findings in 2012 establishing "probable links" between PFAS exposure and kidney cancer, testicular cancer, thyroid disease, ulcerative colitis, and other conditions. Internal manufacturer documents — surfaced through court discovery — show 3M and DuPont knew about PFAS toxicity by the 1970s. They kept selling the foam anyway. They never warned firefighters.

## Who Qualifies for an AFFF Lawsuit in Dallas?

You may qualify to file a claim in the federal AFFF MDL if you can show three things.

**Documented exposure.** You worked with or around AFFF foam. That includes Dallas Fire-Rescue firefighters, DFW Airport ARFF (aircraft rescue and firefighting) personnel, military firefighters at any North Texas base, civilian airport workers, refinery and chemical-plant firefighters, fire instructors, and fire equipment technicians. Training records, service records, employment files, and even witness statements from coworkers can document exposure.

**A qualifying diagnosis.** The federal AFFF MDL recognizes several cancers and disorders linked to PFAS exposure. The most common qualifying diagnoses are:

- Kidney cancer
- Testicular cancer
- Pancreatic cancer
- Thyroid cancer (and certain thyroid diseases)
- Liver cancer
- Ulcerative colitis

**A medical link.** A treating or evaluating physician will need to provide a "nexus opinion" — a written professional opinion connecting your specific diagnosis to your specific PFAS exposure history. Your attorney coordinates this evaluation. You do not pay for it out of pocket.

If you served as a Dallas firefighter between the early 1970s and the late 2010s and were diagnosed with one of these conditions, you may have a claim. Family members of deceased firefighters whose deaths were linked to a qualifying condition may also file.

## DFW-Specific AFFF Exposure Sites

Dallas and the surrounding region have one of the heaviest historical AFFF footprints in Texas. If you worked at any of the sites below, your exposure was likely both prolonged and intense.

**DFW International Airport.** DFW opened in 1974 and used AFFF as the primary aviation firefighting agent from day one. Every ARFF crew member at DFW for the next four decades trained with AFFF in live-fire drills, often several times per year. ARFF training pits and burn pads at major airports are some of the most heavily contaminated AFFF sites in the country.

**Dallas Fire-Rescue.** Dallas Fire began using AFFF in the 1970s on fuel-fire calls, refinery responses, and live-burn training at the Dallas Fire-Rescue Academy. Engine companies that responded to vehicle fires on Central Expressway, I-35E, I-635, and the LBJ often used Class B foam at the scene.

**Dallas Love Field.** A second commercial airport in the city center, Love Field maintained its own ARFF crew that trained with AFFF for decades.

**Naval Air Station Dallas (Hensley Field).** This decommissioned naval air station in west Dallas/Grand Prairie was a major AFFF training and use site through the 1990s. PFAS contamination at and around Hensley Field is well documented.

**Surrounding municipal departments.** Garland, Mesquite, Irving, Carrollton, Richardson, Plano, Lancaster, DeSoto, and Cedar Hill all maintained Class B foam capability for industrial and highway fires. Mutual-aid responders who worked DFW Airport or Hensley Field incidents also had exposure.

**Industrial sites.** Refineries, chemical plants, and large fuel-storage facilities throughout the Dallas region kept AFFF on hand. Industrial firefighters and brigade members at those sites had direct exposure.

## The MDL Process: What Happens When You File

Every federal AFFF personal injury lawsuit in the country has been consolidated into MDL 2873 before Judge Richard M. Gergel in the District of South Carolina. An MDL — a Multidistrict Litigation — is not the same as a class action. In a class action, everyone gets lumped together and you usually get a small share of a single settlement. In an MDL, each plaintiff still has an individual case with individual damages. The court coordinates pretrial work — discovery, expert testimony, manufacturer document production — so the same battles do not have to be fought thousands of times.

The MDL then selects a small group of representative cases called "bellwether trials." Those trials test how juries respond to the evidence and help both sides predict what cases are worth. After bellwethers, manufacturers usually agree to a global settlement framework — but every plaintiff is still evaluated on the strength of their own facts: type of cancer, exposure history, age at diagnosis, lost income, and medical costs.

You do not have to travel to South Carolina. You do not have to give a deposition unless your case is selected as a bellwether. The whole case is built on documents, medical records, and expert opinions. Most AFFF firefighter cases will be resolved through settlement, not trial.

## Texas Statute of Limitations for AFFF Cases

Texas has a two-year statute of limitations on personal injury claims under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. That sounds short, and for most car wrecks it is. But AFFF cases work differently because of the **discovery rule**.

Under the discovery rule, the two-year clock does not start ticking on the day you were exposed. It starts on the day you knew — or reasonably should have known — that your injury was linked to AFFF. For most firefighter cancer cases, that means the clock started when you (or your treating doctor) connected your diagnosis to your PFAS exposure history. For many firefighters, that connection was not even possible until 2016 or later, when the scientific and legal community started linking firefighter cancers to AFFF in a way the general public could see.

Translation: many older diagnoses — going back into the 1990s and 2000s — still qualify under the discovery rule. Do not assume your case is time-barred without talking to a lawyer first. ## What Compensation Could Cover

A successful AFFF claim can recover:

- **Past medical bills** — chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, hospital stays, imaging, follow-up care
- **Future medical costs** — ongoing oncology, monitoring, potential recurrence treatment
- **Lost wages** — the income you missed during treatment and recovery
- **Lost earning capacity** — when cancer or its treatment permanently affects your ability to work
- **Pain and suffering** — the physical and emotional toll of the diagnosis and treatment
- **Loss of consortium** — for spouses, the loss of companionship and household contribution
- **Wrongful death** — for families of firefighters who have died from a qualifying cancer

AFFF cases are **products liability** cases, not medical malpractice cases. That means the Texas medical-malpractice damage caps do not apply. Compensation is determined by your actual losses, the strength of the science in your case, and the defendants' willingness to settle versus go to trial.
