## When to ask — the 3 review moments

### Moment 1 — First positive milestone (Week 2-6 after sign-up)
**Trigger:** Client received first medical care, settlement demand sent, OR client has expressed satisfaction in a recent call.
**Why:** Captures emotional high before the long quiet middle of a case. Some firms wait until close, missing months of goodwill.
**Who asks:** Intake coordinator or paralegal who's been most responsive to the client.

### Moment 2 — Settlement offer (whenever it lands)
**Trigger:** Insurance company makes any settlement offer (even an unacceptable lowball).
**Why:** Client feels validated that the case is moving. Even if they reject the offer, the moment itself is positive.
**Who asks:** Jim or whoever delivered the offer news.

### Moment 3 — Case close / check delivery (mandatory)
**Trigger:** Settlement check delivered or final disbursement complete.
**Why:** Maximum gratitude moment. Every closed case = one review opportunity that we should never miss.
**Who asks:** Whoever hands over the check or makes the final call. Jim if he's available.

## How to ask — the scripts

### English script (in-person or phone)

> "Before we wrap up, can I ask one favor? Our firm runs on word of mouth and Google reviews. People who got hurt like you did are searching online right now for a lawyer they can trust. If you have 60 seconds, would you mind leaving us a Google review about your experience?
>
> I'll text you the link right now. You don't have to write a novel — just a few sentences about [whatever they specifically valued: communication, the result, how we treated them]. Whatever's true for you.
>
> Thanks so much. It means everything to us."

### Spanish script (Mexican vernacular)

> "Antes de terminar, ¿puedo pedirle un favor? Nuestra oficina funciona por boca a boca y por las reseñas de Google. Otras personas como usted están buscando ahora mismo a un abogado en quien confiar. Si tiene 60 segundos, ¿nos podría dejar una reseña en Google sobre su experiencia con nosotros?
>
> Le mando el enlace por mensaje de texto ahora mismo. No tiene que escribir mucho — solo unas cuantas oraciones sobre [lo que más valoró: la comunicación, el resultado, cómo lo tratamos]. Lo que sea verdadero para usted.
>
> Muchísimas gracias. Significa mucho para nosotros."

### Script rules
- **Always ask in the language the client uses.** Spanish client = Spanish ask. Bilingual client = whichever language feels more natural in the conversation.
- **Never script-read.** This is a conversation. Personalize the "whatever they specifically valued" line.
- **Don't beg or apologize for asking.** Direct ask, polite, move on.
- **Never offer anything in exchange.** No gift cards, no fee discounts, no "we'll waive X" — that's a Google ToS violation and can torch the entire GBP.

## The SMS / email templates (sent immediately after the ask)

### English SMS (sent within 60 seconds of the verbal ask)

> Hi [First name] — this is [your name] from Goff Law. Thanks again for trusting us with your case. Here's the link to leave us a Google review when you have a minute: https://g.page/r/[GOOGLE_REVIEW_DIRECT_LINK]
>
> Some ideas if you're not sure what to write:
> • Did we communicate well throughout?
> • Did Jim personally call you back?
> • Were we fast to respond?
> • Did we treat you like family, not a case number?
> • Are you happy with the result?
>
> Whatever's true. Thank you. — [Your name]

### Spanish SMS (Mexican vernacular)

> Hola [First name] — soy [your name] de Goff Law. Gracias otra vez por confiar en nosotros. Aquí está el enlace para dejarnos una reseña en Google cuando tenga un momento: https://g.page/r/[GOOGLE_REVIEW_DIRECT_LINK]
>
> Algunas ideas si no sabe qué escribir:
> • ¿Nos comunicamos bien con usted?
> • ¿Jim le devolvió las llamadas personalmente?
> • ¿Respondimos rápido?
> • ¿Lo tratamos como familia, no como un número de caso?
> • ¿Está contento con el resultado?
>
> Lo que sea verdad. Muchas gracias. — [Your name]

### SMS rules
- **Send within 60 seconds of the verbal ask.** Memory and motivation decay fast.
- **Personal sender name in the message.** "This is Chelsea from Goff Law" beats "Goff Law" — it's a person asking, not a firm.
- **Include the 5-6 idea bullets.** Working-class clients often want to leave a review but don't know what to say. Idea bullets remove the friction.
- **One follow-up text, 7 days later, if no review.** Beyond that, drop it — three asks turns a reasonable request into harassment.

## Responding to reviews — the SOP

### 5-star review (default response, 24-hour SLA)

> Thanks so much, [First name]. It means a lot that you took the time to share this. We're glad we could be there when you needed us. — Jim

### 5-star review with named details (e.g., mentions Jim, mentions a specific result)

Personalize. 2-3 sentences. Reference what they said. Sign as Jim if Jim handled the case.

### 4-star review

> Thank you, [First name]. We appreciate the review and the feedback. If there's something we could have done better, I'd love to hear it — please give me a call directly at (972) 928-0085 anytime. — Jim

### 3-star or below

**Do NOT respond publicly with anything substantive.** Triggers:

1. Pull the case file immediately. Understand the complaint context.
2. Jim makes a private outreach call within 24 hours.
3. Public response is short and de-escalating: "I'm sorry your experience didn't meet your expectations. I'd like to learn more — please reach me directly at (972) 928-0085. — Jim"
4. NEVER argue the facts publicly. NEVER reveal confidential case information. NEVER litigate the review.

### 1-star with no text / spam review
Flag for Google removal via GBP. Don't respond.

### Spanish-language review
Respond IN SPANISH. Same SLA, same warmth, same Jim sign-off.

## What to NOT do (Google ToS + ethics)

- **No fake reviews.** Don't ask employees, friends, family who weren't real clients.
- **No incentives.** No gift cards, no fee discounts, no "we'll buy you lunch."
- **No review gating.** Don't filter clients to ensure only happy ones get the link. Ask everyone equally; let the rating reflect reality.
- **No removing real negative reviews via fake DMCA / fake spam reports.** Texas Bar will care if this gets caught.
- **No buying reviews from third-party services.** Most are detected and penalized.
- **No "review swap" with other firms.** Doesn't happen as openly in legal as in restaurants, but flag if anyone proposes it.
