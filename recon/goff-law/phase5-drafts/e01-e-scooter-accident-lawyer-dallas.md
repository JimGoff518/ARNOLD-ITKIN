# E-Scooter Accident Lawyer in Dallas

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

If you got hit by a Lime, Bird, or Spin scooter on a Dallas sidewalk — or if you were the one riding when a car cut you off in Deep Ellum — you are dealing with a new kind of injury case that most personal injury firms in Dallas are not equipped for. As an **e-scooter accident lawyer in Dallas**, I handle the messy liability and coverage questions that decide who actually pays. This page walks you through how these cases work, what insurance applies, and what to do next.

## How E-Scooter Injuries Happen in Dallas

E-scooter crashes in Dallas fall into two big buckets, and the answers look different depending on which side of the handlebars you were on.

**If you are a pedestrian:** The most common scenario is a scooter riding fast on a sidewalk, blowing through a crosswalk, or weaving in and out of foot traffic. Riders often have no helmet, no light at night, and no training. We see broken hips, head injuries, and torn rotator cuffs from the impact and the fall.

**If you are the rider:** Riders get hurt when a car door swings open without warning, when a driver makes a right turn across a bike lane without looking, when the scooter's front wheel catches a pothole or grate, or when the brakes or throttle fail. We also see riders thrown when a scooter's firmware suddenly cuts speed on a hill or in a "slow zone."

Common Dallas locations for these wrecks:

- **Deep Ellum** — narrow sidewalks, heavy nightlife, distracted drivers on Main, Elm, and Commerce.
- **Uptown** — bar district along McKinney Avenue, valet traffic, and ride-share pickup chaos.
- **Lower Greenville** — pedestrian-heavy entertainment strip with limited bike infrastructure.
- **Bishop Arts District** — narrow Oak Cliff streets, crowded sidewalks, and street parking conflicts.
- **SMU / Highland Park area** — students riding to class, residential streets without bike lanes.
- **Downtown Dallas** — high-rise pedestrian traffic, scooters parked in walkways, scooter-vs-DART-rail crossings.

According to TxDOT, pedestrian fatalities in Texas have climbed every year for the past decade. E-scooters add a new layer to that pedestrian-safety problem because riders move faster than walkers but slower than cars, and they often use spaces that were never designed for them.

## The Insurance Coverage Problem (Why These Cases Are Different)

Here is the part where most generic personal injury firms get stuck. **An e-scooter crash has at least four possible insurance pots, and three of them usually try to deny coverage.**

**1. The scooter company's coverage.** Lime, Bird, and Spin all carry liability insurance, but the policy is usually nominal and the user agreement you tapped through on the app contains a waiver that they will use against the rider. The companies argue the rider is solely responsible. Pedestrians who get hit have a slightly better path here because they never signed the user agreement.

**2. The rider's own auto insurance.** Most personal auto policies exclude motorized vehicles that are not registered to the driver. A scooter rented from an app is almost never covered.

**3. The rider's homeowners or renters insurance.** This is the one most people miss. Homeowners and renters policies sometimes have a personal liability section that covers injuries the policyholder causes to others. But many policies exclude motorized vehicles or "premises-only" injuries. You have to read the actual policy.

**4. The at-fault driver's auto insurance** (if a car was involved). This is usually the cleanest path for an injured rider — same rules as any other car wreck.

**5. The City of Dallas.** If your crash was caused by a known pothole, broken pavement, or missing signage, there may be a claim against the city. Texas governmental-immunity rules are strict and the notice deadlines are short — sometimes as little as 45 to 90 days under city charter — so this path closes fast if you wait.

The reason a generic PI firm misses money for clients in these cases is they only chase the obvious defendant. We chase every available coverage layer.

## Who's Liable in a Dallas E-Scooter Crash?

Liability is rarely on just one party. A typical Dallas e-scooter case can name multiple defendants:

- **The scooter rider** — for riding on a sidewalk where prohibited, riding while intoxicated, exceeding posted speed, or running a signal.
- **The vehicle driver** — for failing to yield, opening a door without looking, distracted driving, or right-hook turns across a bike lane.
- **The scooter company** — for defective brakes, throttle malfunction, firmware errors that caused sudden acceleration or deceleration, or negligent maintenance of the fleet.
- **The City of Dallas or TxDOT** — for known dangerous road conditions, missing or obscured signage, or improperly designed crossings.
- **A property owner** — if the scooter was parked in a way that created a tripping hazard on private property.

Texas is a "modified comparative fault" state under Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 33.001. You can recover damages as long as you are less than 51% at fault. If you are partially responsible, your recovery is reduced by your percentage. That makes apportionment fights in scooter cases very common, and they can swing the value of your case by tens of thousands of dollars.

**Dallas scooter ordinances change frequently.** The City of Dallas has tightened, loosened, and re-tightened dockless scooter rules multiple times since 2018. We pull the operative ordinance for the date of your wreck — not the version that is currently on the city's website.

## Evidence That Disappears in E-Scooter Cases

This is the section that makes or breaks the case. **Every piece of evidence in a scooter crash sits on a corporate retention schedule, and most of it gets wiped in 30 to 90 days.**

- **Scooter ride data** — GPS pings, speed logs, brake events, and battery telemetry are stored by Lime, Bird, and Spin on their servers. Most operators retain ride data for a limited window before purging or anonymizing it. We send spoliation letters within the first week.
- **App account history** — start and stop timestamps, route, and rider ID. Same retention problem.
- **The physical scooter** — after a crash, the scooter often gets picked up by a "juicer" or contractor and put back in service. Brake pads, throttle, and firmware version can be lost forever. We send a preservation demand for the specific scooter ID.
- **Vehicle dashcam and event-data recorder** — modern cars store about 20 seconds of pre-crash data on the EDR. If the at-fault driver's car gets repaired or totaled, that data is lost.
- **Nearby surveillance** — restaurants, bars, valet stands, and apartment buildings in Deep Ellum, Uptown, and Bishop Arts run their own cameras. Most overwrite in 7 to 30 days. We canvass within the first 48 hours.
- **911 audio and dispatch logs** — public records, but you have to file a Texas Public Information Act request quickly because some agencies destroy audio after 90 days.
- **Witness statements** — phone numbers go stale, people move, memories fade. We lock these down in writing the first week.

Every day you wait is evidence you will never see again.

## What To Do Right Now If You're Injured

If you were just hit or just crashed:

1. **Get medical care within 72 hours**, even if you "feel okay." Adrenaline masks soft-tissue injuries, concussions, and disc damage for hours or days. Insurance adjusters use the gap between the crash and your first doctor's visit to cut claim value.
2. **Document everything.** Photograph the scooter, your injuries, the scene, any vehicle involved, and the surrounding intersection. Re-shoot bruising for seven days.
3. **Get the scooter ID.** Every Lime, Bird, and Spin has a unique alphanumeric tag. Photograph it. This is how we subpoena ride data.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the scooter company's insurer, the driver's insurer, or any "claims investigator" who calls. You are not legally required to talk to them.
5. **Do not sign anything** from the scooter company — especially not a release, a "settlement" check, or a broad medical authorization.
6. **Call a lawyer who handles e-scooter cases specifically.** Spoliation letters need to go out in days, not weeks.

## Texas Statute of Limitations

Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003 gives you **two years from the date of the crash** to file a personal injury lawsuit. That sounds like a lot of time. It is not. Insurance negotiations, medical treatment, and lien resolution routinely take 12 to 18 months. If the deadline passes, your case is gone — no matter how strong it was.

If your claim involves the City of Dallas (street defect, signage, sidewalk), the **government-notice deadline is much shorter** than two years and is governed by the Texas Tort Claims Act and the Dallas city charter. Miss it and your case against the city is barred even if your two-year window is still open.
