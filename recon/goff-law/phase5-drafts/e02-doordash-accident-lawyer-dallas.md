# DoorDash Accident Lawyer in Dallas

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

If you were hit by a DoorDash driver in Dallas, this is not a normal car wreck. As a **DoorDash accident lawyer in Dallas**, I deal with a specific problem these cases have: DoorDash's $1 million liability policy only turns on during one narrow window — when the driver (a "Dasher") has accepted an order and is actively delivering it. Outside that window, you may be facing a driver whose personal auto policy specifically excludes commercial use. This page explains exactly how DoorDash's tiered coverage works, how the insurance companies fight over which policy applies, and what you have to do in the first 30 days to lock down which tier was active at the moment of the crash.

## How DoorDash Crashes Happen in Dallas

DoorDash drivers behave differently than ordinary motorists, and that drives a specific crash pattern.

**Time pressure.** Dashers are paid per delivery, and tips are heavily influenced by delivery speed. That creates an incentive to speed, run yellow lights, and ignore stop signs in residential neighborhoods. We see a lot of these wrecks during the dinner rush (5 to 9 p.m.) and weekend lunch.

**Phone-mounted distraction.** Every Dasher is running the DoorDash driver app, GPS navigation, sometimes a second food-delivery app at the same time, and often messaging with the customer. The mounted phone is constantly pinging with new orders, route changes, and customer texts. Looking at the screen for two seconds at 35 mph means the driver travels 100 feet without watching the road.

**Unfamiliar neighborhoods.** Dashers go wherever the algorithm sends them. They are routinely driving through Lakewood, Oak Cliff, M Streets, or Plano subdivisions they have never seen, looking at house numbers instead of the road.

**Vehicle condition.** Dashers use their own cars. Many drive high-mileage vehicles with worn brakes, bald tires, and overdue maintenance because the gig pay barely covers the wear.

**Common crash types we see:** rear-ends at stoplights when the Dasher was looking at the app, side-impacts at residential intersections, U-turns in front of oncoming traffic, parking-lot pedestrian strikes at restaurant pickup, and right-of-way violations near apartment complex entrances.

## The Insurance Coverage Problem (Why These Cases Are Different)

This is the section that makes DoorDash cases different from any other Texas car wreck. **DoorDash's insurance is tier-based, and the tier in effect at the exact moment of impact decides whether you face a $1M policy or a $30,000 minimum-limits personal policy that may not pay at all.**

DoorDash's coverage works in three tiers:

**Tier 1 — Driver is Offline (not logged into the Dasher app):** The driver is just running personal errands. DoorDash provides **zero coverage**. The only available insurance is the driver's personal auto policy. Texas minimum limits are $30,000 per person / $60,000 per accident / $25,000 property. That is often not enough to cover even a single ER visit.

**Tier 2 — Driver is Online but has not accepted an order:** The Dasher app is open and the driver is waiting for an order. DoorDash carries **limited contingent liability coverage** that kicks in if the driver's personal policy denies the claim. This contingent coverage is much narrower than the active-delivery policy and has its own deductibles and conditions.

**Tier 3 — Driver has accepted an order and is actively delivering:** This is the window where DoorDash's **full $1 million occurrence liability policy** is active. The policy covers bodily injury to third parties (you), property damage, and contingent collision coverage for the Dasher's own vehicle (with a deductible).

**The fight in every DoorDash case is proving which tier was active.** A Dasher who just dropped off an order and is on the way home is Tier 2, not Tier 3. A Dasher who is "waiting for an order" but had not yet tapped "Accept" is Tier 2, not Tier 3. The difference is the difference between $30,000 and $1,000,000 in available coverage.

**The deeper problem — the personal-auto commercial-use exclusion.** Almost every standard Texas personal auto policy contains an exclusion for "use of the vehicle to carry persons or property for compensation or a fee" or "while engaged in any business or occupation involving the transportation of persons or property." Translation: when the Dasher is delivering, **their own personal auto policy may legally deny the claim**. That is exactly what happens. The personal insurer denies. The Dasher is on the hook personally. The injured party (you) is left chasing whichever tier of DoorDash coverage was active.

This is why a general PI firm in Dallas often leaves money on the table in these cases. They settle against the personal auto policy at $30K because they did not develop the evidence to push DoorDash into the $1M tier.

## Who's Liable in a Dallas DoorDash Crash?

Multiple parties are usually in play:

- **The Dasher** — directly negligent for the conduct that caused the crash (distracted driving, speeding, running a signal, fatigue).
- **The Dasher's personal auto insurer** — if the personal policy does not have a commercial-use exclusion, or if the crash falls in a window where the exclusion does not apply.
- **DoorDash, through its tier-based commercial policy** — for whichever tier was active at the moment of impact.
- **A restaurant or merchant** — in rare cases where unsafe pickup-zone design contributed to the crash.
- **Another vehicle** — if a third driver shared fault. Texas comparative-fault rules apply under Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 33.001.

The legal theory we usually develop is **negligence by the Dasher plus vicarious commercial coverage through DoorDash's active-delivery policy**. DoorDash takes the position that its Dashers are independent contractors, so direct liability against the company itself is harder. But the commercial liability policy is contractual — once we prove the tier, the policy pays.

## Evidence That Disappears in DoorDash Cases

Every piece of evidence in a DoorDash crash is on a corporate retention schedule, and most of it gets wiped in 30 to 90 days.

- **Dasher app status and timestamps** — the single most important data point. DoorDash's servers record exactly when the Dasher went online, accepted, picked up, and delivered. **This is the evidence that decides which tier was active.** DoorDash retains this data, but a spoliation letter has to go out within days to lock it down.
- **GPS pings and route data** — DoorDash's app pings the Dasher's location continuously during a delivery. We use this to reconstruct speed, route, and exact location at impact.
- **Customer order details** — the merchant, pickup time, drop-off address, and customer name are all platform records.
- **Phone records** — texts between Dasher and customer, app notifications during the relevant window.
- **The Dasher's vehicle EDR (event-data recorder)** — modern cars store about 20 seconds of pre-crash data. If the vehicle gets repaired or scrapped, that data is gone.
- **Nearby surveillance** — restaurants, apartment complex entrances, and commercial parking lots in Dallas neighborhoods overwrite footage in 7 to 30 days.
- **CR-3 crash report** — usually available 10 business days after the wreck from TxDOT.

The DoorDash spoliation letter is the most time-sensitive document in this case type. Every day of delay is a day closer to that tier evidence being purged or anonymized.

## What To Do Right Now If You're Injured

1. **Get medical care within 72 hours.** Soft-tissue injuries, concussions, and disc damage often do not show symptoms for a day or two. Adrenaline hides it. Insurance adjusters use the gap between the crash and your first doctor's visit to discount your case.
2. **Ask the driver if they were on a delivery.** Get the answer at the scene if you can, but do not get into an argument about it. The platform data will prove the tier later.
3. **Photograph the Dasher's car interior if visible** — a mounted phone running the DoorDash app, a food bag on the passenger seat, or a hot-bag in the back is direct evidence of an active delivery.
4. **Get the police to the scene.** A CR-3 with the officer's notes about a "food delivery driver" makes the tier fight easier.
5. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the Dasher's insurance, DoorDash's insurer, or any "claims investigator." You are not legally required to talk to them.
6. **Do not sign a broad medical authorization.** They only need records related to this crash.
7. **Call a lawyer who knows DoorDash's tier structure.** The spoliation letter needs to go out fast.

This page is about consumers and other motorists hit BY a DoorDash driver. If you are a delivery driver who was hit while delivering (Amazon Flex, DoorDash, Uber Eats, Instacart), see our [Injured Gig Driver page](/practice-areas/gig-driver-injury-lawyer-dallas/) — your case is built differently.

## Texas Statute of Limitations

Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003 gives you **two years from the date of the crash** to file a personal injury lawsuit. That deadline applies to claims against the Dasher and against DoorDash's insurance policy.

But the practical deadline is much sooner. DoorDash's app-status and GPS records are on a retention schedule that may be as short as 60 to 90 days for some data fields before they get archived or anonymized. The spoliation demand has to go out in the first weeks, not the last month.

For more on Texas filing deadlines, see our [Statute of Limitations guide](/faq/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-texas/).
