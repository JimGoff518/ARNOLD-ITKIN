## Why Denton Drivers Need an Experienced PI Lawyer

Denton is one of the most unusual traffic markets in North Texas, because it is two cities at once: a university town with tens of thousands of students from the **University of North Texas (UNT)** and **Texas Woman's University (TWU)**, and a commuter and freight hub at the literal top of the I-35 corridor — the place where I-35E and I-35W converge north of the city and split south of it.

The **I-35E/I-35W interchange just north of Denton** is the single most important traffic feature in the city. Everything coming up from Dallas, Lewisville, and Carrollton funnels into Denton at this split. Long-haul 18-wheelers running between DFW and Oklahoma City hit Denton every day. Mix that with university traffic and you get one of the highest-risk freight-and-passenger blends in the state.

**US-380** runs east-west across the south side of Denton and is one of the most crash-heavy state highways in Denton County. **US-377** comes in from the southwest. Loop 288 (the inner loop around east and south Denton) carries heavy local traffic to Golden Triangle Mall, Rayzor Ranch, and the medical district.

Then there is the **student-driver factor**. With UNT enrollment over 45,000 and TWU adding several thousand more, Denton has a massive population of young drivers — many of them new to the area, driving older cars, often without full insurance coverage. That means more uninsured-motorist and underinsured-motorist (UM/UIM) claims in Denton than in most DFW suburbs. If you were hit by a college student with minimum-limits insurance, your own UM/UIM coverage is probably the difference between full recovery and a token payout. Most people do not even realize they carry it. We help you find it.

Denton also has **Denton Municipal Airport (Denton Enterprise Airport)** on the west side of town off Airport Road, which brings general aviation and the supporting commercial traffic — fuel trucks, freight haulers, hangar suppliers — into the western corridor.

A Denton car accident lawyer who actually knows the city understands these patterns. We do.

## Denton County Court System: What You Need to Know

Denton is the **county seat** of Denton County. The **Denton County Courts Complex at 1450 East McKinney Street in Denton** is where almost every personal injury lawsuit filed in Denton County ends up. If you live in Denton, your case is filed in your own city. That cuts down on travel for mediations, depositions, and hearings — and it gives a lawyer who works in this building real local efficiency.

The complex hosts the Denton County district courts (which handle most PI cases above the $250,000 threshold) and the county courts at law (which handle smaller PI cases). Denton County juries tend to bring a mix of perspectives — university-town residents lean one way, rural-corner residents lean another. The cases that win in this courthouse are the ones built carefully, with clean medical records, credible plaintiffs, and prepared witnesses. That is how we build them.

If your wreck happened on the very south end of Denton County — say, in Lewisville or Flower Mound — the case is still filed at the Denton County Courts Complex in Denton, not in Dallas. Many drivers assume otherwise. They are wrong, and it matters for filing strategy.

Most Denton wreck victims get treated at one of these facilities:

- **Medical City Denton** — on West Oak Street near I-35E. Full ER, ICU, surgical services, neurology.
- **Texas Health Presbyterian Hospital Denton** — on West University Drive near TWU. Active ER, orthopedic and neurosurgical capacity, designated stroke and trauma services.

Severely injured patients — major head trauma, spinal cord injuries, multi-system trauma — may be transferred to higher-level trauma centers, including **Medical City Plano** or **Baylor University Medical Center in Dallas**, which carry the highest trauma-level designations in the region. If you were transferred from a Denton ER to a Plano or Dallas trauma facility, that record is critical evidence. It documents that local resources were not sufficient — which speaks directly to the severity of your injuries. We pull those transfer records early in every case.

If you do not have health insurance — and a lot of UNT and TWU students do not, or are on limited student plans — call us before you skip the specialist follow-up. There are ways to get the imaging, the orthopedic consult, and the physical therapy you need without paying out of pocket up front. Gaps in your treatment hurt your case. We help you avoid them.

## Texas Statute of Limitations (Brief Mention)

In Texas, you generally have **two years from the date of the crash** to file a personal injury lawsuit. That deadline comes from Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. There are exceptions — claims against government entities can require notice in as little as 6 months, and claims involving minors can follow different rules — but two years is the default.

Do not wait. Even with two years on the clock, evidence disappears in weeks. We have a full breakdown on our [Statute of Limitations FAQ page](/faq/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-texas/).
