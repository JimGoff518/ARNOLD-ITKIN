# I-635 / LBJ Freeway Accident Lawyer in Dallas

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

Across all Dallas County interstates — and I-635 is one of them — TxDOT recorded **81 fatal crashes and 90 deaths in 2024**, with **27 fatal commercial motor vehicle crashes** across all Dallas County roads that year. I-635, the LBJ Freeway loop through northern Dallas County, sits at the crossroads of every major freight artery in the metro: I-35E, US-75, I-30, and I-20 all feed into it. If you were hit on I-635 — by an 18-wheeler, a delivery truck, or a passenger vehicle — this page explains why the corridor is uniquely dangerous, who can be held liable, and what evidence has to be locked down right away.

## Why I-635 is Especially Dangerous for Trucks

I-635, the LBJ Freeway, is the northern half-loop around Dallas County. It intersects I-35E at the LBJ/I-35E interchange (one of the most reconstructed in DFW history), crosses US-75 at the High Five, runs east through Mesquite to meet I-30, and ties into I-20 at its southern end. Every major north-south and east-west freight corridor in DFW touches I-635 somewhere. That makes it the most crossed-and-merged interstate in the metro — and merge points are where truck crashes happen.

The corridor also carries one of the highest commuter volumes in DFW. Drivers from Plano, Richardson, Garland, and Mesquite all use I-635 as their primary east-west route across the north side. They share the road with tractor-trailers running through DFW on cross-country hauls, regional drayage trucks moving freight between distribution centers, and delivery vehicles serving north Dallas. The mix of long-haul trucks and dense urban commuter traffic, all crossing each other at high-volume interchanges, creates the classic crash conditions: high speed differentials, sudden lane changes, and short merge zones.

I-635 includes the **LBJ Express managed-lanes segment** between the I-35E interchange and US-75 — a separate set of tolled lanes with their own design distinct from the un-managed general-purpose lanes. The managed-lane design changes weave patterns, entry/exit geometry, and traffic mix in that segment. Note: TxDOT does not publish corridor-specific or managed-lane-specific crash counts in its free annual reports, so we don't claim a number we can't source. The design distinction is corridor context, not a crash-data claim.

## Common Causes of Truck Crashes on I-635

The truck cases I see from I-635 in north Dallas fall into a handful of consistent fact patterns:

- **Unsafe lane changes near the LBJ/I-35E interchange, the High Five at US-75, and the I-635/I-30 split.** Truck drivers changing lanes without verifying blind spots cause sideswipe, override, and underride crashes at every one of those interchanges.
- **Sudden stops in stop-and-go traffic.** I-635 backs up during every commute window. Fully loaded tractor-trailers need significantly more stopping distance than a passenger car, and rear-end crashes into stopped commuter traffic are one of the most common patterns.
- **Driver fatigue and Hours-of-Service violations.** Drivers running through Dallas on long-haul corridors often hit I-635 at the end of their daily 11-hour driving limit under [49 CFR § 395.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395/subpart-B/section-395.3). Fatigued reaction times produce missed brake-light cues and lane drift.
- **Brake and tire failure from skipped maintenance.** Texas Transportation Code § 644 adopts the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations requiring carriers to inspect and maintain brakes, tires, and steering. Skipped inspections shift liability from the driver to the carrier.
- **Confusion at managed-lane entry and exit points.** The LBJ Express managed-lanes design uses dedicated entry/exit slip ramps. Drivers — especially commercial drivers unfamiliar with the corridor — sometimes attempt unsafe last-second lane changes to reach an exit, with predictable results.

## Crash Data: What TxDOT Tells Us About Dallas County

Honest framing first: TxDOT publishes free crash data by county and road type, but does not publish a free, downloadable report that isolates I-635 from the other Dallas County interstates. The numbers below are the most precise corridor-relevant figures TxDOT publishes for free.

- **81 fatal crashes and 90 deaths in 2024 across all Dallas County interstates** combined (I-20, I-30, I-35E, I-45, I-345, I-635). Source: [TxDOT 2024 Fatal Crashes by Road Type, Table 12](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/12.pdf).
- **27 fatal commercial motor vehicle crashes and 29 deaths in Dallas County in 2024** across all roads, with **3,857 total CMV-involved crashes** that year. Source: [TxDOT 2024 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf).
- **Dallas + Tarrant counties combined = 8.4% of all Texas fatal commercial truck crashes in 2024** (46 of 546 statewide). Source: [TxDOT 2024 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf).

TxDOT does not publish I-635-specific fatal counts in free reports — for verified per-corridor or per-mile-marker statistics, contact us during your free consultation and we can request a custom dataset through the TxDOT CRIS Query Tool. TxDOT defines "Commercial Motor Vehicle" as any vehicle over 10,000 lbs gross weight, a broader bucket than just 18-wheelers but the cleanest free public proxy.

## Who's Liable When a Crash Happens on I-635

Truck and large-vehicle crashes on I-635 rarely involve only the driver. Multiple parties usually have legal exposure before the truck or commercial vehicle ever leaves the yard:

- **The driver** — for negligent driving, HOS violations, distracted driving, or impairment.
- **The motor carrier** — for negligent hiring, failure to train, failure to maintain equipment, or pressuring drivers to exceed HOS limits.
- **The cargo loader or shipper** — for overloading, improper securement, or hazardous-materials violations.
- **The maintenance contractor** — for skipped or negligent brake, tire, or steering work.
- **The truck or component manufacturer** — for defective brakes, tires, steering, or electronic stability systems. Defective-component cases sometimes feed federal MDL proceedings.
- **For some I-635 managed-lane crashes, additional parties may be relevant** — toll-road operator signage practices, lane-marking maintenance, or third-party contractor responsibility can come into play depending on the facts.

Federal regulations require motor carriers to maintain minimum liability insurance ranging from $750,000 (general freight) to $5,000,000 (certain hazardous materials). A catastrophic I-635 crash with permanent injuries or wrongful death can exceed those minimums fast. Identifying every layer of corporate liability is how you reach the layered insurance coverage that actually pays for catastrophic damages.

## Evidence That Disappears Within Days of an I-635 Crash

The trucking company's defense team — and the insurance carrier's investigators — are mobilizing within hours of any serious I-635 crash. Their job is to control the evidence. Yours, through your lawyer, is to lock it down first. The fastest-disappearing items:

- **Electronic Logging Device (ELD) data.** Federal law under [49 CFR § 395.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395/subpart-B/section-395.22) only requires carriers to retain ELD records for 6 months. "Lost" or "corrupted" ELD data shows up repeatedly in truck litigation.
- **Engine Control Module (ECM) / "black box" data.** Modern truck ECMs record speed, throttle position, brake application, and steering input in the seconds before a crash. If the truck is repaired or scrapped, that data is destroyed.
- **Dashcam footage.** Many fleets operate forward-facing and driver-facing cameras with auto-overwrite cycles as short as 7 days.
- **Driver Qualification File and prior HOS logs.** Federal law requires the carrier to maintain a file documenting medical certifications, road tests, and prior violations. These files often expose negligent-hiring patterns.
- **Post-crash drug and alcohol test results.** FMCSA requires post-crash testing within 8 hours (alcohol) and 32 hours (drugs) for any fatality or recordable crash.
- **TxDOT highway camera footage along I-635, LBJ Express managed-lane operator data, and surveillance footage from gas stations, restaurants, and businesses along the corridor.** These commonly overwrite on cycles of 72 hours to 30 days.

A formal **spoliation letter — sent within the first 30 days** — puts the carrier on legal notice that destroying this evidence exposes them to sanctions and adverse-inference jury instructions. For managed-lane crashes, a parallel preservation request to the toll-road operator is often necessary to lock down lane-usage and entry/exit data.

## What To Do If You're in a Crash on I-635

1. **Call 911 immediately.** Any wreck involving a commercial truck on I-635 needs a police response and a CR-3 crash report.
2. **Get safely clear of moving traffic.** I-635 lanes move at highway speeds and the managed-lane segment has limited shoulder access; never stand in a lane to take photos.
3. **Photograph everything.** Truck DOT number on the cab door, all trailer placards (especially any hazmat diamonds), license plates, debris field, skid marks, scene wide-shots, lane-marker context (especially in the managed lanes), and your own injuries.
4. **Get medical care within 72 hours.** Adrenaline masks soft-tissue, head, and disc injuries. A clean medical record from the first 72 hours is one of the biggest factors in claim value.
5. **Do NOT give a recorded statement to the trucking company's insurance.** They will call within 24 hours. Tell them to direct all communication to your attorney.
6. **Call a lawyer the same day.** ELD data, dashcam footage, and surveillance video on I-635 start disappearing within a week.
