# I-35 Truck Accident Lawyer in Dallas

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

Across all Dallas County interstates, TxDOT recorded **81 fatal crashes and 90 deaths in 2024** — and I-35E, the north-south freight backbone running from the Mexican border through downtown Dallas to Oklahoma, is one of the busiest interstates in that count. If you or someone you love was hit by an 18-wheeler on I-35E, this page explains what makes this corridor uniquely dangerous, who can be held liable, and what evidence we have to lock down fast before the trucking company "loses" it.

## Why I-35E is Especially Dangerous for Trucks

I-35E is the Dallas leg of the original NAFTA Superhighway. From Laredo at the Mexican border, freight rolls north on I-35 through Austin, splits at Hillsboro into I-35E (Dallas) and I-35W (Fort Worth), then rejoins at Denton on its way to Oklahoma City, Kansas City, and the Great Lakes. That makes I-35E one of the highest-volume tractor-trailer routes in the United States.

Inside Dallas County, the corridor includes the Stemmons Freeway segment north of downtown — one of the oldest sections of urban Interstate in Texas — plus the downtown Dallas Mixmaster (where I-35E, I-30, and US-75 collide in a stack of overpasses), and the LBJ interchange where I-635 cuts across. Every one of those features is a known truck-crash hot spot for the same reasons: tight curves, short merge ramps, lane drops, and traffic stacking up at every commute window.

Pile on the volume problem. Eighteen-wheelers running through DFW on I-35E share lanes with daily commuters from Lewisville, Carrollton, and the Park Cities. Speed differentials between a fully loaded 80,000-lb tractor-trailer and a sedan slowing for traffic produce the kind of rear-end and underride crashes that kill car occupants and leave the truck driver with a scratch. The corridor's age, its design, and its freight volume combine to make I-35E one of the corridors I treat with the most urgency when a call comes in.

## Common Causes of Truck Crashes on I-35E

A few causes show up over and over on I-35E truck cases:

- **Driver fatigue.** Long-haul drivers running the I-35 corridor from the border are often deep into their federal Hours-of-Service limits under [49 CFR § 395](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395) by the time they hit Dallas. Fatigued drivers misjudge stopped traffic at the Mixmaster, drift between lanes on Stemmons, and miss brake-light cues at the LBJ interchange.
- **Speeding and following too closely.** A loaded tractor-trailer needs roughly 40% more distance to stop than a passenger car. On I-35E's congested urban segments, that math fails constantly.
- **Unsafe lane changes near merges.** Stemmons Freeway has merge ramps that drop traffic directly into traveling lanes with little room. Trucks changing lanes without checking blind spots cause sideswipe and override crashes here repeatedly.
- **Brake failure and maintenance shortcuts.** Texas Transportation Code § 644 adopts the federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations, which require carriers to inspect and maintain brakes, tires, and steering. When a carrier skips that work to keep a truck on the road, the resulting crash is the carrier's responsibility — not just the driver's.
- **Cargo shift and overloaded trailers.** Improperly loaded cargo on a curve at the Mixmaster or the LBJ stack can roll a trailer. The loading company often shares liability with the carrier.

## Crash Data: What TxDOT Tells Us About Dallas County

Honest framing first: TxDOT publishes free crash data by county and road type, but does not publish a free, downloadable report that isolates I-35E from the other Dallas County interstates. The numbers below are the most precise corridor-relevant figures TxDOT publishes for free.

- **81 fatal crashes and 90 deaths in 2024 across all Dallas County interstates** combined (I-20, I-30, I-35E, I-45, I-345, I-635). Source: [TxDOT 2024 Fatal Crashes by Road Type, Table 12](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/12.pdf).
- **27 fatal commercial motor vehicle crashes and 29 deaths in Dallas County in 2024** across all roads, with 3,857 total CMV-involved crashes. Source: [TxDOT 2024 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf).
- **Dallas + Tarrant counties combined = 8.4% of all Texas fatal commercial truck crashes in 2024** (46 of 546 statewide). Source: [TxDOT 2024 Crash Facts, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf).

TxDOT defines "Commercial Motor Vehicle" as any vehicle over 10,000 lbs gross weight or designed to transport more than 8 passengers — that's a broader bucket than just 18-wheelers, but it's the cleanest publicly available proxy. For corridor-specific I-35E numbers, TxDOT requires an interactive query through their CRIS tool. If you need exact mile-marker or sub-type counts for your specific crash, we can pull them during your free case review.

## Who's Liable When a Truck Crash Happens on I-35E

Truck cases are not car cases with bigger numbers. The liability picture is fundamentally different because multiple companies are usually involved before the crash ever happens. Any of the following may share legal responsibility:

- **The driver** — for negligent driving, Hours-of-Service violations, or driving impaired.
- **The motor carrier** (the trucking company) — for negligent hiring, failure to train, failure to maintain equipment, or pressuring drivers to exceed HOS limits.
- **The cargo loader or shipper** — for overloading, improper securement, or hazardous-materials violations.
- **The maintenance contractor** — if a third-party shop performed (or skipped) the brake or tire inspection that caused the failure.
- **The truck or component manufacturer** — for defective brakes, steering, tires, or electronic stability systems. These are the cases that sometimes feed into federal MDL proceedings.

Carriers carry minimum federal liability coverage of $750,000 to $5 million depending on cargo type, but a serious I-35E crash with permanent injuries or wrongful death can blow past those limits fast. Identifying every responsible party early is how you reach the layered insurance towers that actually pay for catastrophic damages.

## Evidence That Disappears Within Days of an I-35E Truck Crash

The trucking company's lawyers are on the phone with their carrier within hours of any serious I-35E crash. Their job is to control the evidence. Yours is to lock it down before it disappears. The most time-sensitive items:

- **Electronic Logging Device (ELD) data.** Federal law requires most commercial trucks to use an ELD that records driving time, on-duty time, and engine-off time. Carriers are only required to retain ELD records for **6 months** under [49 CFR § 395.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395/subpart-B/section-395.22) — and "lost" data is a recurring theme in truck litigation.
- **Engine Control Module (ECM) / "black box" data.** Modern trucks record speed, throttle position, brake application, and steering input in the seconds before a crash. If the truck gets repaired or scrapped, that data is gone.
- **Dashcam footage.** Many fleets run forward-facing and driver-facing cameras that auto-overwrite on a 7- to 30-day cycle.
- **Driver Qualification File and HOS logs.** Federal law requires the carrier to keep a file documenting medical certifications, road tests, and prior violations. We pull these to expose negligent-hiring patterns.
- **Post-crash drug and alcohol test results.** FMCSA requires post-crash testing within 8 hours (alcohol) and 32 hours (drugs) for any fatality or DOT-recordable crash.
- **Surveillance video from nearby businesses, gas stations, and TxDOT cameras** along I-35E. These overwrite on cycles as short as 72 hours.

A formal spoliation letter — sent within the first 30 days — puts the carrier on legal notice that destroying this evidence will expose them to sanctions and adverse-inference jury instructions later. That single letter is one of the most valuable things a lawyer does in the first month of an I-35E truck case.

## What To Do If You're in a Truck Crash on I-35E

1. **Call 911 immediately.** Even if you think the crash is minor, any wreck involving a commercial truck on I-35E needs a police report and a CR-3.
2. **Get clear of moving traffic if it is safe to do so.** I-35E has fast-moving freight in every lane; do not stand in a lane to take photos.
3. **Photograph everything.** Truck DOT number on the cab door, trailer placards (especially hazmat), license plates, scene wide-shots, debris, skid marks, and your injuries.
4. **Get medical care within 72 hours.** Adrenaline masks soft-tissue and head injuries for days. A clean medical record from the first 72 hours is critical to the value of your claim.
5. **Do NOT talk to the trucking company's insurance adjuster.** They will call within 24 hours, they will sound friendly, and anything you say will be used to reduce your claim. Tell them to call your lawyer.
6. **Call a truck accident lawyer the same day.** ELD data, dashcam footage, and surveillance video start disappearing within a week.
