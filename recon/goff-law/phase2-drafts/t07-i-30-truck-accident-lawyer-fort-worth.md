# I-30 Truck Accident Lawyer in Fort Worth

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

Fatal commercial truck crashes in Tarrant County **jumped 58% from 2022 to 2024**, according to TxDOT — from 12 fatal CMV crashes in 2022 to 19 in 2024, the highest count in the three-year data window. That trend runs counter to the slight statewide decline in overall fatalities, and Fort Worth's busiest east-west freight corridor — I-30 — is squarely inside the geography driving that increase. If an 18-wheeler hit you on I-30 in or near Fort Worth, this page explains what makes this corridor uniquely dangerous, who can be held liable, and what evidence has to be locked down right away.

## Why I-30 is Especially Dangerous for Trucks

I-30 is the primary east-west connector between Fort Worth and Dallas — over 30 miles of interstate through Tarrant County alone. It runs straight through the urban core: downtown Fort Worth, the West 7th corridor, the entertainment district north of AT&T Stadium and Globe Life Field in Arlington, and onward through Grand Prairie into Dallas County. That puts a high-volume freight artery directly through Tarrant County's most densely populated stretch.

The corridor's geography compounds the risk. I-30 intersects I-35W and I-820 in Fort Worth, then crosses I-635 and I-35E in Dallas County. Every one of those interchanges is a documented merge-and-weave crash point. The Arlington stretch carries event-driven surge traffic on Cowboys and Rangers game days, when commuter and tourist drivers mix with through-running tractor-trailers. Downtown Fort Worth's segment has tight curves and short ramp geometry that pre-dates modern truck design.

Tarrant County is also home to several major distribution and logistics centers feeding regional drayage trucks onto I-30 throughout the day. Combine that with long-haul I-30 traffic running between Texarkana, Little Rock, and Memphis, and you get a corridor with a constant mix of regional and long-haul truck traffic — two very different driving cultures sharing the same lanes at highway speeds. That mix is one reason Tarrant County's CMV fatal count is rising even as overall traffic deaths are flat or declining.

## Common Causes of Truck Crashes on I-30

The cases I see from I-30 in Fort Worth tend to share a handful of fact patterns:

- **Driver fatigue, especially on long hauls.** Drivers running I-30 from Memphis or Little Rock often hit Fort Worth at the end of their daily 11-hour limit under [49 CFR § 395.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395/subpart-B/section-395.3). Fatigued reaction times produce rear-end crashes into stopped traffic at the Mixmaster, West 7th, and Arlington.
- **Speeding through urban segments.** I-30 through downtown Fort Worth and the Arlington stadium area was not designed for modern 80,000-lb tractor-trailers at 70 mph. Stopping distances exceed the available sight lines.
- **Unsafe lane changes near I-35W, I-820, and I-635.** Truck drivers changing lanes without verifying blind spots at these interchanges cause sideswipes, overrides, and underride crashes.
- **Brake and tire failure from skipped maintenance.** Texas Transportation Code § 644 adopts the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations requiring carriers to inspect and maintain brakes, tires, and steering. Carriers that skip those inspections to keep equipment running shift liability onto themselves.
- **Event-traffic crashes near AT&T Stadium and Globe Life Field.** Surge traffic on game days produces sudden traffic-flow changes that trucks cannot respond to as quickly as passenger vehicles.

## Crash Data: What TxDOT Tells Us About Tarrant County

Honest framing first: TxDOT publishes free crash data by county and road type, but does not publish a free, downloadable report that isolates I-30 from the other interstates in Tarrant County. The numbers below are the most precise corridor-relevant figures TxDOT publishes for free.

- **Tarrant County fatal commercial motor vehicle crashes rose 58% from 2022 to 2024** — from 12 fatal CMV crashes (13 deaths) in 2022 to 19 fatal CMV crashes (19 deaths) in 2024, the highest count in the three-year data window. Source: [TxDOT 2024 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf) and [TxDOT 2022 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2022/30.pdf).
- **Across all Tarrant County interstates combined, TxDOT recorded 50 fatal crashes and 59 deaths in 2024.** Source: [TxDOT 2024 Fatal Crashes by Road Type, Table 12](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/12.pdf).
- **Tarrant County recorded 1,716 total commercial motor vehicle crashes in 2024** across all roads. Source: [TxDOT 2024 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf).
- **Dallas + Tarrant combined produced 46 fatal CMV crashes in 2024 — 8.4% of all Texas fatal commercial truck crashes** (46 of 546 statewide). Source: [TxDOT 2024 CMV Crashes by County, Table 30](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/30.pdf).

TxDOT defines "Commercial Motor Vehicle" as any vehicle over 10,000 lbs gross weight — broader than just 18-wheelers, but the cleanest publicly available proxy. For I-30-specific mile-marker data or crash sub-types, TxDOT requires an interactive query through their CRIS tool. We can pull a custom dataset for your specific crash during your free case review.

The 58% rise in Tarrant fatal CMV crashes is the trend Fort Worth drivers should know about. The corridor is moving the wrong direction.

## Who's Liable When a Truck Crash Happens on I-30

Truck cases are not car cases scaled up. The liability structure is fundamentally different because multiple companies usually have legal exposure before the truck ever leaves the yard:

- **The driver** — for negligent driving, HOS violations, distracted driving, or impairment.
- **The motor carrier** — for negligent hiring, failure to train, failure to maintain equipment, or pressuring drivers to exceed federal HOS limits to hit a delivery window.
- **The cargo loader or shipper** — for overloading, improper securement, or hazardous-materials violations.
- **The maintenance contractor** — for skipped or negligent brake, tire, or steering work.
- **The truck or component manufacturer** — for defective brakes, tires, steering, or stability-control systems. Defective-component cases sometimes feed federal MDL proceedings.

Federal regulations require motor carriers to maintain minimum liability insurance ranging from $750,000 to $5,000,000 depending on cargo type. Catastrophic crashes on I-30 — multi-vehicle pileups, wrongful death, traumatic brain injury — can exceed those minimums fast. Identifying every layer of corporate liability is how you reach the insurance coverage that actually pays for catastrophic injuries.

## Evidence That Disappears Within Days of an I-30 Truck Crash

The trucking company's defense team is mobilizing within hours of any serious I-30 crash. Their job is to control the evidence. Yours — through your lawyer — is to lock it down first. The fastest-disappearing items:

- **Electronic Logging Device (ELD) data.** Federal law under [49 CFR § 395.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-395/subpart-B/section-395.22) only requires carriers to retain ELD records for 6 months. "Lost" ELD data shows up repeatedly in truck litigation.
- **Engine Control Module (ECM) / "black box" data.** Modern truck ECMs record speed, throttle position, brake application, and steering input in the seconds before a crash. If the truck is repaired or scrapped, that data is gone.
- **Dashcam footage.** Many fleets operate forward-facing and driver-facing cameras that auto-overwrite on a 7- to 30-day cycle.
- **Driver Qualification File and prior HOS logs.** Federal law requires the carrier to maintain a file documenting medical certifications, road tests, and prior violations. These files often expose negligent-hiring patterns.
- **Post-crash drug and alcohol test results.** FMCSA requires post-crash testing within 8 hours (alcohol) and 32 hours (drugs) for any fatality or recordable crash.
- **TxDOT highway camera footage, City of Fort Worth and City of Arlington traffic cameras, and surveillance footage from stadium-area businesses and gas stations along I-30.** These commonly overwrite on cycles of 72 hours to 30 days.

A formal **spoliation letter — sent within the first 30 days** — puts the carrier on legal notice that destroying this evidence exposes them to sanctions and adverse-inference jury instructions. That letter is one of the highest-value documents produced in the early life of an I-30 truck case.

## What To Do If You're in a Truck Crash on I-30

1. **Call 911 immediately.** Any wreck involving a commercial truck on I-30 needs a police response and a CR-3 crash report.
2. **Get safely clear of moving traffic.** I-30 lanes move freight at highway speeds; never stand in a lane to take photos.
3. **Photograph everything.** Truck DOT number on the cab door, all trailer placards (especially any hazmat diamonds), license plates, debris field, skid marks, scene wide-shots, and your own injuries.
4. **Get medical care within 72 hours.** Adrenaline masks soft-tissue, head, and disc injuries. A clean medical record from the first 72 hours is one of the biggest factors in claim value.
5. **Do NOT give a recorded statement to the trucking company's insurance.** They will call within 24 hours. Tell them to direct all communication to your attorney.
6. **Call a truck accident lawyer the same day.** ELD data, dashcam footage, and surveillance video on I-30 start disappearing within a week.
