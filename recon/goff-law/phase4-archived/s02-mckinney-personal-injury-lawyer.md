## Why McKinney Drivers Need an Experienced PI Lawyer

McKinney is one of the fastest-growing cities in America, and it shows in the traffic. US-75 (Central Expressway) is the main north-south spine through the city and one of the busiest commuter corridors in Collin County. The interchange where US-380 meets US-75 — right at the edge of downtown McKinney — handles tens of thousands of vehicles a day, and serious wrecks there are routine.

US-380 itself is a problem corridor. The east-west stretch from west McKinney into east McKinney has been expanding for years, with construction, lane shifts, and shifting speed limits. State DOT data has flagged US-380 as one of the most crash-prone roads in North Texas, and the McKinney segment is no exception.

Then add the local arteries. Eldorado Parkway, Virginia Parkway, Stonebridge Drive, and Custer Road all carry heavy daily traffic in and out of the master-planned communities — Stonebridge Ranch, Adriatica Village, Tucker Hill, and Craig Ranch (just over the line into Collin County's southwest corner). Downtown McKinney brings in destination traffic to the historic square and the dining district almost every weekend.

McKinney also has its own airport — McKinney National Airport — on the east side off SH 5. General-aviation activity and the supporting industrial traffic on Industrial Boulevard add commercial vehicle exposure most drivers do not think about.

All of this means McKinney drivers face crash risk that a generic "Dallas-area" lawyer will not understand at the same depth as someone who knows these specific roads, intersections, and traffic patterns.

## Collin County Court System: What You Need to Know

McKinney is the **county seat** of Collin County. That matters more than people realize. The Collin County Courthouse — the **Russell A. Steindam Courts Building at 2100 Bloomdale Road in McKinney** — is where almost every personal injury lawsuit filed in Collin County ends up. If you live in McKinney, your case is filed in your own city. That cuts down on travel, makes mediations and hearings easier to attend, and gives a local lawyer working in this building every week a real edge.

The Collin County Courthouse hosts the district courts (which handle most personal injury cases above the $250,000 threshold), the county courts at law (which handle smaller PI cases), and family court. The clerks know how documents should be formatted, which judges run tight dockets, and what mediation timing works in this courthouse. That institutional knowledge matters.

Collin County juries tend to be conservative on damages but receptive to clean, well-organized evidence and credible plaintiffs. They reward preparation and they punish exaggeration. A McKinney personal injury case has to be built carefully — not as a billboard pitch, but as a courthouse-ready file. That is how we build them.

## Texas Statute of Limitations (Brief Mention)

In Texas, you generally have **two years from the date of the crash** to file a personal injury lawsuit. That deadline comes from Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. There are exceptions — claims against government entities can require notice in as little as 6 months, and claims involving minors follow different rules — but two years is the default.

Do not wait. Even with two years on the clock, evidence disappears in weeks. We have a full breakdown on our [Statute of Limitations FAQ page](/faq/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-texas/).
