## Why Plano Drivers Need an Experienced PI Lawyer

Plano is one of the most concentrated corporate hubs in the country. **Toyota Motor North America's headquarters** sits at Legacy West. **Frito-Lay's corporate headquarters** is on Legacy Drive. **JC Penney's longtime headquarters** sits in the same corridor. Add Liberty Mutual, Dr Pepper Snapple, FedEx Office, and dozens more — and you get one of the densest commuter traffic loads in DFW every weekday morning and evening.

The roads carrying that traffic are unforgiving. **US-75 (Central Expressway)** is the city's main north-south spine and one of the busiest commuter corridors in Texas. **The Dallas North Tollway** runs the western edge through Legacy West. **President George Bush Turnpike (SH 190)** cuts across the south side. **Spring Creek Parkway, Park Boulevard, Legacy Drive, and 15th Street** all carry heavy local-to-corporate traffic — and the intersections at **Park & US-75, Legacy & the Tollway, and Spring Creek & Coit** produce serious wrecks every month.

Then there is the Plano demographic factor. Median household income in Plano runs well above the Texas average, which means more corporate executives, more two-car households, and more comprehensive insurance policies — particularly **uninsured-motorist and underinsured-motorist (UM/UIM) coverage** on policies that often run to $250,000, $500,000, or higher. UM/UIM coverage matters more than most people realize. It is the policy that pays *you* when the other driver does not have enough insurance to cover your medical bills, lost wages, and pain. Most Plano residents carry significant UM/UIM and do not even know it. We help you find it and use it.

Plano ISD has more than 70 campuses, which means school-zone crashes, drop-off-line fender-benders, and teen-driver wrecks every week. **Plano police make heavy use of body cams and dashcam footage** — which is excellent evidence in a PI case if you know how to request it before it gets overwritten.

A Plano personal injury lawyer who actually knows the city understands these patterns. We do.

## Collin County Court System: What You Need to Know

Plano sits in Collin County. If your case ends up filed as a lawsuit, it will be filed at the **Russell A. Steindam Courts Building at 2100 Bloomdale Road in McKinney**, the Collin County seat. From central Plano it is about 25 to 35 minutes north on US-75 depending on traffic — close enough that mediations, hearings, and depositions are manageable.

A small portion of west Plano crosses into Denton County. If your wreck happened west of the Dallas North Tollway in the right specific area, the case could be filed at the Denton County Courts Complex in Denton instead. We confirm county at intake.

Why does local court familiarity matter? Because every Texas county has its own pace, its own docket habits, and its own jury pool. Collin County juries tend to be conservative on damages but receptive to clean, well-organized evidence and credible plaintiffs. They reward preparation and they punish exaggeration. A lawyer who has worked in the Steindam building knows how clerks like documents formatted, which judges run tight dockets, and how to schedule mediations efficiently. That institutional knowledge matters in a high-value Plano case.

## Texas Statute of Limitations (Brief Mention)

In Texas, you generally have **two years from the date of the crash** to file a personal injury lawsuit. That deadline comes from Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003. There are exceptions — claims against government entities can require notice in as little as 6 months, and claims involving minors can follow different rules — but two years is the default.

Do not wait. Even with two years on the clock, evidence disappears in weeks. We have a full breakdown on our [Statute of Limitations FAQ page](/faq/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-texas/).
