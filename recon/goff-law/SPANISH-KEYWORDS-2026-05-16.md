## Methodology + Confidence Caveat

- **Volume estimates** are derived from Google Keyword Planner-style ranges, Ahrefs/SEMrush patterns observed in DFW Spanish PI SERPs through 2025, and cross-referenced English-volume ratios (Spanish DFW PI queries typically run 8-22% of English volume for the same intent in this market). All numbers below should be treated as **directional estimates** until validated in Ahrefs Lite — anything flagged `[ESTIMATE - verify with Ahrefs Lite]` is lower-confidence.
- **Dialect bias:** DFW Spanish-speakers are ~75% of Mexican origin (Census ACS 2023 DFW MSA), so terminology favors **Mexican Spanish**. Where Caribbean/Central American variants exist (e.g., "carro" vs "auto" vs "coche"), I've noted them. "Choque" (Mexican) outranks "accidente" in some informal queries — both are tracked.
- **Excluded by firm rule:** No keywords for med mal, nursing home, dog bite, pure workers' comp, birth injury, generic slip-fall, SSDI, or defamation regardless of volume. In-lane only: car/truck/motorcycle/rideshare/wrongful death/mass tort/third-party work injury/emerging torts.

## Strategic Notes for Implementation

1. **Niche Stack #4 alignment confirmed.** Spanish × mass tort has the lowest competition-to-volume ratio of any segment in this dataset. Hair relaxer, Paragard, and AFFF Spanish queries are essentially uncontested in DFW SERPs as of recon date — Reyes and McCraw both have token Spanish pages but no mass-tort Spanish content.

2. **FAQ section (Section 3) is the fastest win.** 32 question-format keywords with Low competition and meaningful volume. Featured-snippet capture in Spanish DFW PI SERPs is significantly easier than English. Build these as standalone question pages, not subsections.

3. **Suburb Hispanic concentration matters more than raw suburb size.** Irving (41% Hispanic), Mesquite (47%), Grand Prairie (43%), Garland (40%), and Arlington (29% but largest absolute count) should be prioritized over Frisco/Plano for Spanish-tail pages.

4. **Mexican dialect bias.** "Carro" > "auto" > "coche" in informal queries. "Choque" > "accidente" in colloquial. "Tractocamión" and "tráiler" both used for 18-wheeler. "Relajador" (Mexican) vs "alisador" (broader LatAm) for hair relaxer — track both.

5. **Trust/identity head terms ("abogado hispano", "abogado que hable español") are underweighted by volume but overweighted by conversion.** These are people who have already decided they want a Spanish-speaking lawyer — the rest of the SERP filters out non-Spanish firms automatically.

6. **Code-switching queries** like "abogado para hair relaxer en español" and "abogado hernia mesh español Dallas" are increasingly common among 2nd-gen DFW Hispanic users — don't dismiss as anomalies.

7. **Compliance scope:** Not flagged here per Jim's directive — handled separately.
