# How Much Do Personal Injury Lawyers Charge in Texas?

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

If you are wondering how much personal injury lawyers charge in Texas, here is the short answer: $0 upfront, and a percentage of the final recovery only if we win. The standard Texas sliding scale is **33⅓% before a lawsuit is filed, 40% once a lawsuit is filed, and 45% if the case goes to appeal**. Beyond that fee, there are case expenses — and how those get handled is the most important part of any fee agreement you sign.

## Contingency fees explained: $0 upfront, paid only if you win

A contingency fee means the lawyer is paid only if they recover money for you. You do not write a check for an hourly rate. You do not pay a retainer. If we take your case and recover nothing, you owe us nothing for our time.

This system exists for a reason. Most people hurt in a Texas car wreck or other negligence claim cannot afford to pay $400 to $700 per hour to a private attorney — and even if they could, paying out of pocket for two years before any recovery would bankrupt them. Contingency aligns the lawyer's incentive with yours: we both win when you win.

The mechanics are simple. When the case settles or a jury awards money, the gross recovery flows into the law firm's trust account. From that account, three things come out, in order: case expenses, the attorney's fee, and any medical liens or subrogation claims. Whatever remains is your net check.

## The standard 33⅓% / 40% / 45% sliding scale in Texas

Most Texas personal injury firms use a sliding scale that ties the fee percentage to how much work was required to recover the money. The standard scale:

- **33⅓% (one-third)** — if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed. Pre-litigation cases involve demand letters, claim negotiation, and resolving with the insurance company directly.
- **40%** — if a lawsuit has to be filed because the insurance company will not pay fair value voluntarily. Litigation involves discovery, depositions, motions, and trial preparation — substantially more time and overhead.
- **45%** — if the case goes through trial and then to appeal. Appellate work is specialized, time-intensive, and requires additional briefing and oral argument.

Some firms use different breakpoints (35% pre-suit / 40% post-suit, or 40% across the board). Some specialty cases — federal mass torts, complex commercial trucking, products liability — may have different structures because of how the fees get court-approved and split among co-counsel.

Whatever the scale, **the fee percentage should be written into your fee agreement before you sign**, and you should never be surprised at the end of the case. If a lawyer is vague about the percentage on day one, that is a red flag.

## What "case expenses" cover and who pays them

Case expenses are different from attorney's fees. Expenses are the actual costs of building your case — money paid to third parties, not to the law firm. Common case expenses in a Texas personal injury matter include:

- Filing fees and court costs.
- Medical records and bills (often $25 to $200 per provider).
- Police reports and crash reconstruction.
- Expert witness fees (treating physicians, accident reconstructionists, life-care planners, economists — often $5,000 to $25,000 each).
- Deposition transcripts.
- Mediation fees.
- Investigation and process service.
- Trial exhibits and demonstrative evidence.

In a contingency case, most reputable Texas firms advance these costs and only get reimbursed if the case recovers. If we recover nothing, we eat the expenses too. That is the deal.

The critical question to ask before you sign: **"Are case expenses deducted before or after the attorney's fee is calculated?"** This single sentence can shift thousands of dollars one way or the other.

Example: $300,000 settlement, $30,000 in expenses, 40% attorney fee.

- Expenses deducted first ("net" model): ($300,000 - $30,000) × 40% = $108,000 attorney fee. Client nets $162,000.
- Fee taken first ("gross" model): $300,000 × 40% = $120,000 attorney fee. Client nets $150,000 after expenses.

The "net" model is more client-friendly. Ask which model your lawyer uses and get the answer in writing.

## The fee agreement: what to read before you sign

The Texas fee agreement (formally called a "contingent fee contract") is required by Texas law to be in writing and signed by the client. Before you sign, read it carefully. Look for these specific things:

1. **The percentage scale.** Is it 33⅓ / 40 / 45 or something different? When does each tier trigger?
2. **Expense handling.** Net model or gross model? Who advances costs? What happens if there is no recovery?
3. **Subrogation and liens.** Who negotiates with health insurance, Medicare, Medicaid, hospital liens, and any ERISA plan? Reducing these liens can put thousands back in your pocket — and that work matters.
4. **Termination clause.** What happens if you fire the lawyer mid-case? Most fee agreements include a quantum meruit clause that allows the prior lawyer to claim a portion of the eventual recovery.
5. **Referral fees.** Was your case referred from another lawyer? If so, Texas allows fee splits with proper disclosure — make sure it does not increase your total fee.
6. **Settlement authority.** The decision to accept or reject any settlement offer is always yours, not the lawyer's. Confirm that is in writing.

Ask questions before you sign. A lawyer who will not patiently explain the contract on day one will not patiently explain anything else later.

## How fees are calculated on a settlement vs. trial verdict

The mechanics work the same way whether the money comes from a settlement check or a jury verdict. The gross recovery is the starting number. From there, expenses come out, the attorney's fee is calculated under the contract, medical liens and subrogation are negotiated and paid, and the client gets the net.

Key differences between settlement and trial recovery:

- **Settlement:** Recovery is fixed and certain. Fee is at the post-suit percentage (typically 40%) if suit was filed; pre-suit percentage (typically 33⅓%) if it settled before filing.
- **Trial verdict:** Recovery is whatever the jury awards. The defense usually appeals large verdicts, which can trigger the higher 45% appellate tier. Verdicts also accrue post-judgment interest, which adds to recovery.
- **Structured settlements:** Some large recoveries are paid out over time. The attorney's fee is typically calculated on the present value of the structure, not the lifetime payout.

The right answer is rarely the highest possible recovery on paper. The right answer is the recovery you can actually get into your hands, after expenses, taxes (most physical injury recoveries are tax-free, but consult a CPA), and lien resolution.

## Why "cheap" lawyers often cost more in the end

Some Texas firms advertise reduced contingency rates — 25%, "low fee guarantee," "we charge less than the other guys." This sounds attractive when you are looking at gross numbers, but it is almost always more expensive in real life. Here is why.

**The fee is a percentage of recovery. The recovery is what matters.** A firm charging 25% on a $100,000 settlement nets you $75,000. A firm charging 40% but settling the same case for $250,000 nets you $150,000 — twice as much, even at a higher fee percentage.

The "cheap" model only works for the law firm if the firm processes a high volume of low-value settlements quickly. That is the opposite of what most clients with serious injuries actually need. If your case has surgery, future treatment, or any complexity, a firm built on volume settlement processing will pressure you to accept the first reasonable offer — because their entire business model depends on closing files fast.

Look for these signs of a firm that will fight for full value:

- A history of taking cases to trial when the insurance company refuses to pay fair value.
- Willingness to advance significant case expenses on complex matters.
- Direct attorney involvement (you talk to a lawyer, not just a case manager).
- Honest answers to "what is my case worth?" — not promises in dollar figures.
- Specific knowledge of your case type, not generic "we do everything" marketing.

The fee percentage matters less than what the lawyer does with your case. Hire on capability, not price.

## When to call a Texas personal injury lawyer

If you have meaningful injuries and the case is more complex than a simple fender-bender with full liability admitted, talk to a personal injury lawyer before signing anything with an insurance company. The consultation is free, the case review costs nothing, and you are not committing to hire anyone by picking up the phone.
