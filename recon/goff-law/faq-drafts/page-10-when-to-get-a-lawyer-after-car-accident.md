# When To Get a Lawyer After a Car Accident in Texas

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

The honest answer to **when to get a lawyer after a car accident** is: **within the first 7 days, almost always.** That is not a sales pitch — it is what the evidence and the insurance adjuster timelines force. Every day you wait, witnesses forget what they saw, vehicles get repaired or scrapped, and surveillance footage gets overwritten. Meanwhile, the at-fault driver's insurance company is moving fast on their side. This guide explains exactly when to call a lawyer, what waiting actually costs you, and why the cost to hire is $0.

## The honest answer: within the first 7 days, almost always

If you were in a Texas car accident and any of these are true, you should call a lawyer this week:

- You went to the hospital or emergency room
- You missed work because of the crash
- You still have pain 72 hours later
- A commercial vehicle (semi-truck, delivery van, rideshare, company car) was involved
- Anyone was killed
- The other driver's insurance has already called you

The reason for "7 days" is simple: it gives a lawyer time to preserve evidence before it disappears, take over communications before you say something that hurts you, and coordinate your medical treatment before billing chaos sets in. After day 7, every one of those things gets harder.

You do not need to be sure you want to sue. You do not need to decide anything in the first call. You just need a real lawyer evaluating your case before the other side has a week's head start.

## The 3 evidence problems that get worse every day you wait

**Problem 1: Witnesses forget.** Eyewitness memory degrades fast. A bystander who saw the crash clearly on day one will give a different account on day fourteen, and a useless one on day sixty. Lawyers preserve witness testimony with sworn statements taken while memory is fresh. Wait too long and the witness either cannot be found, cannot be reached, or no longer remembers details that mattered.

**Problem 2: Vehicles get repaired or scrapped.** Crashed vehicles contain evidence — black-box data (EDR), crush patterns, brake-component condition, tire wear, airbag deployment timing. Once the vehicle is repaired or sent to salvage, that evidence is gone forever. Tow yards typically release vehicles within 15-30 days. A lawyer sends a **spoliation letter** ordering the tow yard and the insurance company to preserve the vehicle for inspection. Without that letter, the car is fixed and the evidence is lost.

**Problem 3: Surveillance footage gets overwritten.** Gas stations, parking lots, businesses, traffic cameras, doorbell cameras, and apartment complexes near most DFW intersections record continuously — and overwrite their loops on a **30-day cycle**. Some smaller systems overwrite in 7-14 days. If there is footage of your crash, it has to be requested in writing before it is gone. A lawyer knows which businesses on which intersection have cameras and how to demand preservation immediately.

By day 30, two of these three evidence categories may already be unrecoverable. By day 60, often all three.

## Why insurance adjusters move faster than you think

The at-fault driver's insurance company is typically notified within **24 hours** of the crash. By **48 hours**, an adjuster has often already:

- Called the at-fault driver and taken a recorded statement (favorable to them)
- Pulled the police report
- Calculated the case's likely settlement range
- Started trying to reach you for a recorded statement (unfavorable to you)
- Begun looking for reasons to deny or reduce liability

Adjusters call victims early for a reason: you are in pain, you are stressed, you do not yet know the full extent of your injuries, and you have not talked to a lawyer. Anything you say is recorded and used later. Common adjuster traps in the first call:

- "Just confirm you're feeling fine today, right?" — locks in a "no injuries" statement before symptoms develop
- "Can you describe what happened in your own words?" — looking for any phrasing that suggests fault
- "We'd like to send you a quick check for $1,500 to cover the inconvenience" — accepting closes your claim forever

If you have not hired a lawyer by the time the adjuster calls, you are negotiating against a trained professional whose job is to pay you as little as possible — alone, in pain, and unrepresented. That is not a fair fight.

## Specific triggers: when waiting is malpractice on yourself

Some accidents are clearly minor — low-speed fender-benders with no injuries, where one quick conversation with your own insurance handles everything. Many cases do not need a lawyer.

But if any of the following are true, you should not wait another day:

- **You were taken by ambulance or admitted to a hospital.** Hospital bills run $5,000 to $50,000+ for even a short stay. You need someone organizing how those bills get paid.
- **A commercial vehicle was involved.** Trucking companies, rideshare companies, and delivery companies have lawyers on retainer who start investigating within hours. You need someone on your side at the same speed.
- **You were a passenger in someone else's car.** You have potential claims against multiple parties and multiple policies, which gets complicated fast.
- **The other driver was uninsured or fled the scene.** Your case becomes a claim against your own UM/UIM coverage, which is a different and more adversarial process than most people expect.
- **You missed more than one day of work.** Lost wages are recoverable but require specific documentation a lawyer knows how to collect.
- **You are still in pain 72 hours after the crash.** Soft-tissue injuries (whiplash, disc injuries, concussions) often present 24-72 hours after impact. If pain has not resolved, your case is bigger than it first appeared.
- **The other driver's insurance company has already contacted you.** Game already started. You need a lawyer between you and them.
- **Anyone died in the crash.** Wrongful death cases have specific procedural requirements that begin immediately.

## What a lawyer does in the first 48 hours

A good Texas personal injury lawyer is not waiting for you to finish treatment to start working. Within the first 48 hours of being hired, the firm should:

1. **Send a spoliation letter** to the at-fault driver, their insurer, and the tow yard, ordering preservation of the vehicle, EDR data, and any other physical evidence.
2. **Send preservation requests** to nearby businesses for surveillance footage before the overwrite cycle hits.
3. **Take over all communication** with insurance companies — you no longer talk to adjusters, ever.
4. **Pull the crash report** (CR-3) and identify witnesses for early outreach.
5. **Coordinate medical treatment** with providers who understand PI cases and can document injuries properly. Set up Letters of Protection if you have no health insurance.
6. **Open the case file** with photos, statements, medical records request authorizations, and a working timeline.
7. **Notify your own insurance** of the claim if PIP, MedPay, or UM/UIM may apply.

That is real work, completed in two days. The cost to you for all of it: $0. Which brings us to the last point.

## Cost: $0 to call, $0 to hire (contingency)

Texas personal injury lawyers work on **contingency fees**. That means:

- **$0 to call us.** Free consultation. No obligation. No card on file.
- **$0 to hire us.** You sign a contingency agreement, you pay nothing upfront.
- **$0 if we lose.** No recovery, no fee. We eat the case expenses.
- **We get paid only if you recover.** A percentage of the settlement or verdict (typical Texas contingency: 33⅓% pre-suit, 40% after suit is filed, 45% if appeals).

There is no financial reason to wait. Calling a lawyer after a car accident does not commit you to anything. It just gets a real evaluation of your case from someone whose job is to be on your side — not the insurance company's.

If you are wondering whether your case is worth a lawyer's time, the call will answer that question in about 15 minutes. If we cannot help you, we will tell you honestly and refer you to someone who can.
