# How Much Is My Personal Injury Case Worth in Texas?

By [Jim Goff](/attorneys/jim-goff/) | Updated 2026-05-16

The honest answer to "how much is my personal injury case worth" is "it depends" — but I can tell you exactly what it depends on. The value of a Texas personal injury case is the sum of your economic losses, your non-economic losses, and (in rare cases) punitive damages, adjusted for who was at fault and how much insurance is available to pay. Below is the same framework I walk every new client through in their free consultation.

## The honest answer: it depends on these 6 factors

Anyone who quotes you a dollar figure in the first phone call is either guessing or lying. Real case value is driven by 6 things:

1. **Severity of your injury.** A whiplash that resolves in 6 weeks is not the same as a herniated disc requiring surgery. Permanent injury changes everything.
2. **Total medical bills, including future care.** Past treatment plus what future treatment will cost (a future-care life-care plan from a doctor is sometimes needed).
3. **Lost income and lost earning capacity.** Wages missed plus any reduction in what you can earn going forward.
4. **Liability clarity.** Is fault 100% on the other driver, or are you 20% at fault for being on your phone? Texas uses modified comparative fault — over 50% on you and you recover nothing.
5. **Available insurance and assets.** A $5 million case against a driver with a $30,000 policy and no assets is, practically speaking, a $30,000 case unless there is an umbrella policy or third party to pursue.
6. **Venue and jury pool.** A Dallas County jury values cases differently than a Collin County jury — same facts, different number.

The first 3 are about your losses. The last 3 are about whether those losses can actually be collected.

## Economic damages: medical bills + lost wages + future earnings

Economic damages are the bills you can prove with paper. They include:

- **Past medical bills** — ER, surgery, physical therapy, imaging, prescriptions, follow-up visits.
- **Future medical care** — additional surgeries, lifetime PT, durable medical equipment, home modifications, in-home care.
- **Past lost wages** — every shift you missed, documented with pay stubs and an employer letter.
- **Future lost earning capacity** — if your injury limits what you can do for work, an economist calculates the loss over your remaining work life.
- **Out-of-pocket costs** — mileage to appointments, prescription co-pays, medical devices you bought yourself.
- **Property damage** — repair or actual cash value of your vehicle, plus rental car costs.

Economic damages are the floor of your case. Insurance companies fight them by arguing your bills are inflated (Texas allows "paid or incurred" challenges under Civil Practice & Remedies Code § 41.0105), or that some of your treatment was unrelated to the wreck. A good lawyer pushes back with medical records and treating physician statements.

## Non-economic damages: pain, suffering, and Texas's caps

Non-economic damages cover what you cannot put on an invoice:

- Physical pain and suffering, past and future.
- Mental anguish.
- Disfigurement.
- Physical impairment.
- Loss of enjoyment of life.
- Loss of consortium (spouse's claim for impact on the marriage).

**The cap question.** Texas does cap non-economic damages in two specific contexts: medical malpractice ($250,000 against most providers per Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code § 74.301) and claims against government entities (sovereign immunity caps under the Texas Tort Claims Act, usually $250,000 per person / $500,000 per occurrence).

Here is what matters for you: **most Texas personal injury cases have NO cap on pain and suffering.** Car wrecks, truck wrecks, rideshare crashes, defective products, and dangerous-property claims against private businesses are uncapped. We do not handle medical malpractice cases, so for the cases we take, the jury can award what they think is fair — period.

That is a big deal. It means a herniated disc case with $80,000 in medical bills can still be worth $400,000 to $600,000 in the right venue with the right facts. The cap conversation only matters if your case involves a doctor, hospital, or government entity.

## Punitive damages — when Texas allows them

Punitive damages (Texas calls them "exemplary damages") are extra money meant to punish bad behavior, not compensate your losses. Under Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code § 41.003, you have to prove by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant acted with fraud, malice, or gross negligence.

Examples where Texas juries have awarded punitives:
- Drunk drivers, especially repeat offenders.
- Trucking companies that knowingly let unsafe drivers stay on the road.
- Manufacturers that hid a known product defect.

Punitives are capped under § 41.008 at the greater of $200,000 or 2x economic damages plus an amount equal to non-economic damages up to $750,000. So they are limited — but on the right case they meaningfully add to total recovery.

If you smell punitives in your case (drunk driver, distracted commercial truck driver, defective auto part), tell your lawyer on day one. The evidence to support punitives — driver history, internal company emails, prior complaints — has to be locked down early.

## How insurance companies calculate offers (the multiplier method)

Insurance companies use software (the most famous is Colossus) to value claims, but the basic logic is the multiplier method. Here is how it works in plain English:

1. Add up your total economic damages — call this number X.
2. Multiply X by a number between 1.5 and 5, depending on severity. That is the pain-and-suffering value.
3. Add the two together. That is the rough "case value" from the adjuster's side.

A typical mild injury (no surgery, full recovery in months) gets a 1.5x to 2x multiplier. A moderate injury (injections, ongoing therapy, partial recovery) gets 2x to 3x. A severe injury (surgery, permanent impairment, scarring) gets 3x to 5x or more.

Example: $30,000 in medical bills + $10,000 in lost wages = $40,000 economic. At a 3x multiplier, the rough value is $40,000 + $120,000 = $160,000.

**Important caveat:** the multiplier method is a starting point insurance adjusters use. Juries do not use multipliers. Juries award what they think is fair based on the human story. Real trial verdicts often blow past multiplier numbers — which is exactly why insurance companies push hard to settle before trial.

## Why early settlement offers are almost always too low

The first offer you receive from the at-fault driver's insurance company will be a small fraction of your case's real value. There are four reasons.

First, you do not know your damages yet. If you have not finished treatment, you do not know your future medical costs, and you cannot ask for them.

Second, the adjuster is rewarded for closing files cheaply. Their performance reviews measure how little they paid, not how fair the outcome was.

Third, the adjuster is betting you do not know what your case is worth. Lots of people accept a $5,000 check on a $50,000 case because $5,000 sounds like a lot of money when bills are piling up.

Fourth, once you sign the release, the case is over. There are no re-do's. If you find out three months later that you need surgery, you cannot reopen the claim. That single release-signature is the most expensive signature most people ever sign.

Wait until you have either reached maximum medical improvement or you and your doctor know exactly what future treatment looks like. Then negotiate from a position of knowledge.

## The 3 things that increase case value most

After taking and reviewing many Texas PI cases, I can tell you the 3 things that move the needle most:

1. **Documented, consistent medical treatment.** Gaps in treatment kill case value faster than anything. If your doctor recommends 8 weeks of PT, finish all 8 weeks. If you missed appointments because of work or childcare, document why.
2. **Liability evidence locked down early.** Surveillance footage, 911 calls, truck black box data, witness statements — all of it gets harder to obtain (or disappears) within 30 to 60 days. A lawyer involved in week one can preserve everything.
3. **Credible, prepared client.** Juries pay people they believe. Inconsistent statements, social media posts that contradict your injuries, or aggressive demands all reduce value. A client who is calm, honest, and consistent is worth significantly more — same injury, same facts.

Two bonuses: a treating physician willing to testify about causation, and an experienced trial lawyer the insurance company knows will actually take the case to trial if needed. Insurance companies pay differently to lawyers who try cases vs. lawyers who only settle.

## When to call a Texas personal injury lawyer

If you have meaningful injuries — anything that sent you to a doctor, took you out of work, or will require future treatment — call a lawyer before you talk to the at-fault insurance company. Most Texas PI lawyers (including us) offer free consultations and work on contingency. There is no risk to learning what your case might be worth.
