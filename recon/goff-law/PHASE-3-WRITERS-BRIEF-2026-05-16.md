## Why this phase is the moat

This is **the highest-leverage Phase in the 90-day plan** and Jim wants it executed precisely.

Keyword research turned up something important: **no DFW competitor is targeting [federal MDL drug/device] × [DFW city] keyword combinations.** Across our recon of nine DFW competitors (Mullen & Mullen, Thompson, Witherite, Reyes, Hamilton Wingo, Lyons & Simmons, McCraw, Snellings, J. Alexander), zero have city-specific pages for AFFF, hernia mesh, hair relaxer, or Paragard. The only DFW mass-tort keywords with measurable competition are the generic city-level terms — "dallas mass tort lawyer" (~80/mo) and "fort worth mass tort lawyer" (~60/mo) — and even those are weakly targeted.

That means these 10 pages fill **structural whitespace** in the DFW SERP. Each one targets a low-volume but extremely high-intent keyword (someone searching "afff lawsuit lawyer dallas" is not browsing — they have a cancer diagnosis and a phone). Search volumes are 20-45/mo per page. That looks small until you remember: a single signed AFFF or hair relaxer case is worth multiples of an entire year of car-wreck volume.

**Strategic logic in plain English:**

1. We win because no one is competing for these terms in DFW yet — first-mover advantage on combos no one else has bothered to build.
2. We win because the federal MDL framing positions Goff Law against Tier S firms (Aldous, Hamilton Wingo, Reyes) who do NOT have mass-tort practices the same way — they own catastrophic injury or volume PI, not federal MDL drug/device.
3. We win because Texas's medical malpractice tort cap doesn't apply to federal MDL products-liability cases — that distinction (covered in detail below) is the rhetorical hook on every page.
4. We win because the DFW geographic specificity (Dallas hospitals named, Fort Worth bases named, Mesquite roads named) ranks better than the generic national mass-tort firms (Morgan & Morgan, Weitz & Luxenberg, Aylstock) that currently rank #1-3 for generic terms.

**Don't optimize these pages for traffic volume.** Optimize them for the one cancer patient on the couch at 11 p.m. with a phone in her hand. If 30 people see the page and 1 calls, this works. The unit economics on a qualifying mass-tort case make every other Phase look like rounding error.

## Texas tort-cap framing — the rhetorical hook on every page

**Critical for these pages.** The Texas medical malpractice damages cap (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 74.301) caps non-economic damages at **$250,000 per defendant doctor / $500,000 aggregate against multiple healthcare institutions** in medical malpractice cases. That cap was created by HB 4 (2003 tort reform) and is one of the principal reasons Jim does NOT take medical malpractice cases in Texas — the economics don't work for plaintiffs.

**The point of mentioning this on Phase 3 pages: Federal MDL products-liability cases are NOT medical malpractice.** They are products-liability cases against the chemical manufacturer or medical device manufacturer. The Texas medmal cap **does not apply**. That distinction matters enormously to a client trying to decide whether to file.

**Suggested phrasing block** (adapt naturally; don't paste verbatim across all 10 pages):

> Hair relaxer / hernia mesh / AFFF / Paragard cases are **products liability** cases against the manufacturers — not medical malpractice cases against your doctor. That distinction matters in Texas. The state's medical-malpractice damages cap under § 74.301 limits non-economic damages in medmal cases. Federal MDL products cases sit outside that cap. Compensation is determined by your actual losses, the strength of the science in your file, and the manufacturer's settlement posture — not by an artificial Texas cap.

For Amazon DVR pages (M9, M10), the parallel framing is:

> Amazon delivery vehicle cases are **commercial vehicle negligence and corporate liability** claims. They are not medical malpractice, and Texas's medmal damages cap does not apply. In rare cases of egregious conduct (Amazon's documented internal time-pressure policies, for example), **punitive (exemplary) damages** may also be on the table.

Place this paragraph either in the "Who Qualifies" section or in the "What Compensation Could Cover" section. It should appear once per page, prominently, not buried.

**Compliance edge:** Do NOT claim Texas's medmal cap applies to these cases. Do NOT claim no Texas damages cap exists for product cases (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 41.008 caps EXEMPLARY damages, separate from compensatory). Frame ONLY around the medmal cap (§ 74.301) not applying.
