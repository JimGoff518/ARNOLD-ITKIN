## Section 6 — The 5 things Dianne must NEVER do

These come from the Voice Guide + Jim's case-exclusion list. Bake these in from Day 1:

1. **NEVER name competitors.** No "unlike other firms," no "Goff vs. the big billboards," no naming Mullen / Witherite / Reyes / McCraw / Aldous / anyone. Differentiate POSITIVELY (by Goff's advantages), never by attacking others.
2. **NEVER promise results.** No "we'll get you the most money," no "guaranteed settlement," no "we always win." Texas Rule 7.02 sensitivity (Jim handles compliance; Dianne avoids the trap upstream).
3. **NEVER use "best / top-rated / award-winning / premier / elite"** as adjectives. They don't help SEO and create compliance exposure. Use real numbers, real years, real types of cases instead.
4. **NEVER mention excluded case types in CTAs.** Goff does NOT handle: medical malpractice (general), nursing home abuse, dog bite, generic slip-and-fall, workers' comp (pure), birth injury, SSDI, defamation. Disclaimer language on pages is encouraged (filters traffic). But CTAs only cite IN-LANE cases.
5. **NEVER use lawyer-speak.** "Compensation" → "money." "Victim" → "someone who got hurt." "Collision" → "wreck." "Injured" → "hurt." "Plaintiff" → "you." Full banned-words list in Voice Guide §4.

## Section 8 — Expected questions Dianne will ask Jim (pre-answered)

**Q: What's the tone for high-stakes pages like wrongful death?**
A: Voice Guide §6 (Tone calibrations). Quieter, reverent, no brand-slogan headers. "We are so sorry for your loss. When you're ready, we'll be here. Jim takes these calls personally."

**Q: Spanish — should I write it myself or do you have a native speaker?**
A: Phase 1b ES brief recommends a native Mexican-Spanish reviewer pass before publish. If Dianne writes good Spanish, ship her drafts → Jim hires a native reviewer for a final pass. If Dianne isn't a strong Spanish writer, we hire a native Spanish copywriter for Phase 1b separately and Dianne stays in English.

**Q: What if a brief has a fact I can't verify (e.g., DoorDash $1M coverage figure, FMCSA fatigue stat)?**
A: Master checklist has a "fact-verification flags" section — 15 specific items already called out. If Dianne hits one not on the list, flag it in the draft with `<!-- VERIFY: [thing] -->` and Jim resolves before publish.

**Q: Word counts — do I have to hit exactly?**
A: ±200 words is fine. The page-by-page targets in each brief are guidelines, not gates. The Voice Guide voice trumps word count.

**Q: Can I deviate from the suggested H2 outline in the brief?**
A: Yes, if it improves the page. The H2 outline is what survived the strategy pass — but Dianne's writing instinct is welcome. Flag big restructures in the ping to Jim.

**Q: What if I can't find a cross-link target page?**
A: Phase 4 v2 brief assumes some Phase 2 / Phase 3 slugs that may not match what we built. The master checklist already flags this. If a cross-link doesn't exist, leave a TODO comment in the draft — Chelsea fixes during publish.

**Q: Should I write the schema JSON-LD blocks too?**
A: No — Chelsea generates them from your H2s automatically. Just write good H2 questions and the schema falls out of them.

**Q: Texas Rule 7.02 (advertising rules) — do I need to flag concerns?**
A: NO. Jim handles all Texas Disciplinary Rules compliance. Dianne's job is to follow the Voice Guide (which keeps her safely upstream of Rule 7.02 issues). If you're worried about a specific phrase, flag it to Jim; don't research compliance yourself.
