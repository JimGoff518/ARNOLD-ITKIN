## Headline finding: $1B in verdicts SINCE 2023 ALONE — fastest-growing prestige firm in dataset

Lyons & Simmons has a credential profile that on paper looks similar to Hamilton Wingo (catastrophic injury, elite trial credentials) but with **two material structural differences**:

1. **Recent verdict velocity is staggering.** "$1 Billion in Verdicts and Settlements **SINCE 2023**" — a rolling 3-year claim, not lifetime. This is faster cumulative growth than Hamilton Wingo's $7.3B (which is dominated by a single 2022 case).
2. **Geographic strategy is OILFIELD-FOCUSED, not Dallas-focused.** They have 13 location pages including Midland, Odessa, Big Spring, Abilene, and New Mexico — the Permian Basin oil-and-gas corridor — alongside their Dallas HQ and Houston office.

**Implication:** Lyons & Simmons is the **second elite-tier Dallas catastrophic firm** (with Hamilton Wingo), but they aren't competing in the same lane as Hamilton Wingo. Hamilton Wingo owns the prestige-credential battle from a single Dallas office. Lyons & Simmons owns the **multi-region oilfield catastrophic vertical** with offices/presence across West Texas.

**The play:** Lyons & Simmons doesn't have 13 physical offices — they have 13 location pages targeting oilfield-worker injury claims across the Permian Basin oil corridor. Each is a programmatic landing page anchored by their oilfield verdict portfolio ($33.1M oilfield + $25M oilfield + $109.5M gas explosion).

**Why this is smart:** Oilfield cases are HIGH-VALUE catastrophic events with predictable geographic concentration. By owning the SERP for "Midland oilfield accident lawyer" and "Odessa oilfield disaster attorney," they get pre-qualified high-value lead flow.

**Strategic implication for Goff:** Lyons & Simmons does NOT contest DFW suburb territory (Frisco/Plano/Allen/McKinney) — they go after West Texas oilfield. **They are not a direct geographic competitor to Goff's suburb expansion play.** But if Goff ever wants to enter oilfield as a practice area, Lyons & Simmons is the incumbent to dislodge.
