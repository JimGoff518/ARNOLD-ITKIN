## The 2022 Research That Triggered the Federal Litigation

In October 2022, researchers at the National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences (NIEHS) published findings from the Sister Study — a long-running prospective cohort of nearly 34,000 women — that established a statistically significant association between chemical hair relaxer use and uterine cancer. The study, led by Dr. Alexandra White, found that women who used chemical hair relaxers more than four times per year had **more than double the risk of uterine cancer** compared to women who never used these products.

The Sister Study became the scientific foundation for the federal litigation. Within months of publication, the first federal lawsuits were filed. In April 2025, a Special Settlement Master (Ellen K. Reisman) was appointed to begin coordinating settlement negotiations. By April 2026, MDL 3060 in the Northern District of Illinois included more than 11,440 federal cases — a number that grows monthly as more women connect their cancer diagnoses to their hair relaxer use history.

## The Chemicals Implicated in MDL 3060

The federal litigation identifies several categories of endocrine-disrupting chemicals in hair relaxer formulations:

- **Phthalates** (DEHP, DBP) — plasticizers used in fragrance formulations; classified as endocrine disruptors
- **Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives** — used to prevent microbial contamination
- **Parabens** — preservatives with documented endocrine-disrupting properties
- **Cyclosiloxanes** — additives that contribute to product texture
- **Bisphenols** — including bisphenol A (BPA)

These chemicals interfere with the body's hormone signaling and are associated with hormone-driven cancers including uterine, endometrial, and ovarian cancers.

If you are considering a hair relaxer cancer claim and want to start building documentation now, North Texas-specific strategies include:

**Salon records.** If you used the same Black-owned salon for many years, the salon may have client records or appointment history that documents application frequency. Many established Black salons in the DFW suburbs have maintained client records for decades.

Your attorney can help recover documentation you cannot obtain on your own through subpoenas, salon records requests, and beauty supply purchase history research.
