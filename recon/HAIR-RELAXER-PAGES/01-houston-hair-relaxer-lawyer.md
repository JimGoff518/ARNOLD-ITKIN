## How Hair Relaxers Became a National Public Health Crisis for Black Women

Chemical hair relaxers have been sold in the United States for more than a century, but the formulations most relevant to the current litigation came to market in the mid-twentieth century and dominated the Black hair care industry through the 1960s, 1970s, 1980s, 1990s, 2000s, and 2010s. By any measure, this is one of the most prolonged and concentrated chemical exposures any demographic group has experienced in modern consumer products history — and the exposure was specifically marketed to Black women, who used these products at significantly higher rates than any other demographic group.

The fundamental chemistry that made hair relaxers effective also made them dangerous. To break down the disulfide bonds in tightly coiled hair, manufacturers used a combination of harsh active ingredients (sodium hydroxide, calcium hydroxide, guanidine carbonate) plus a category of chemicals called endocrine-disrupting chemicals — substances that interfere with the body's hormone signaling. Many hair relaxer formulations also contained or released phthalates (especially DEHP and DBP), parabens, formaldehyde-releasing preservatives, cyclosiloxanes, and a class of compounds called bisphenols. Several of these chemicals are now classified as known or suspected endocrine disruptors, and several are now known to be specifically associated with hormone-driven cancers.

### The 2022 NIH Sister Study That Changed Everything

In October 2022, researchers at the National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences (NIEHS) published findings from the Sister Study — a long-running prospective cohort of nearly 34,000 women — that established a statistically significant association between chemical hair relaxer use and uterine cancer. The study, led by Dr. Alexandra White, found that women who used chemical hair relaxers more than four times per year had **more than double the risk of uterine cancer** compared to women who never used these products.

The Sister Study did what decades of consumer safety advocacy and individual case reports had not done: it provided the kind of large-scale prospective epidemiological evidence that courts and the FDA take seriously. Within months of publication, the first federal lawsuits were filed. By April 2025, a Special Settlement Master had been appointed to begin coordinating settlement negotiations. By April 2026, more than 11,440 federal cases were pending in MDL 3060 in the Northern District of Illinois.

### What the Manufacturers Knew

Discovery in the federal litigation has begun surfacing internal documents from the major manufacturers showing decades of internal awareness about endocrine-disrupting chemicals in hair care products. The legal theory in MDL 3060 includes design defect, failure to warn, fraudulent concealment, negligence, breach of warranty, and unjust enrichment. The core allegation is that L'Oréal and the other defendants knew or should have known about the cancer risks of their formulations, yet continued marketing these products — disproportionately to Black women and women of color — without adequate warnings.

## Who Qualifies to File from Houston

You may qualify to file a hair relaxer cancer lawsuit if all four of the following apply to you or a deceased family member:

1. **Documented or recalled use of chemical hair relaxers.** Use over multiple years is the typical pattern — most claimants used relaxers for ten or more years. The use does not need to be continuous, and you do not need to remember every brand. Mixed brand use is common and acceptable. Salon applications, store-bought kits, and at-home applications all qualify equally.

2. **Qualifying medical diagnosis.** Most commonly uterine, endometrial, or ovarian cancer. Other reproductive system cancers and conditions may also qualify; speak with an attorney before assuming your specific diagnosis does not.

3. **Medical nexus.** A treating or evaluating physician's documented opinion linking your diagnosis to your chemical hair relaxer use. Your attorney coordinates this evaluation as part of building the claim.

4. **Timely filing under statute of limitations.** Texas's two-year statute of limitations runs from the date of diagnosis OR from the date you knew or should have known the cancer was linked to hair relaxers (the discovery rule). Because the link was not widely understood until the 2022 NIH study, the discovery rule often extends the filing window for older diagnoses. Do not assume your claim is time-barred without speaking with an attorney.

Family members of deceased women whose deaths resulted from a qualifying cancer may file wrongful death and survival action claims on behalf of their loved one.
