## What Texas Women Need to Know About Hair Relaxer Cancer Risks

The 2022 NIH Sister Study by Dr. Alexandra White established a statistically significant association between chemical hair relaxer use and uterine cancer. The study, which followed nearly 34,000 women, found that women who used chemical hair relaxers more than four times per year had **more than double the risk of uterine cancer** compared to women who never used these products. For Texas women whose use pattern began in childhood (relaxer kits applied by mothers or older sisters at home) and continued through adulthood, the cumulative use over a thirty- or forty-year lifespan typically far exceeds this threshold.

The chemicals implicated include:

- **Phthalates** (DEHP, DBP) — endocrine-disrupting plasticizers used in fragrance formulations
- **Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives** — used to prevent microbial contamination in product
- **Parabens** — preservatives with documented endocrine-disrupting properties
- **Cyclosiloxanes** — additives that contribute to product texture and feel
- **Bisphenols** — including bisphenol A (BPA) and related compounds

These chemicals interfere with the body's hormone signaling, and several are now classified as known or suspected endocrine disruptors with documented associations to hormone-driven cancers including uterine, endometrial, and ovarian cancers.

## Texas-Specific Statute of Limitations Considerations

Texas's two-year statute of limitations runs from the date of diagnosis OR from the date you knew or should have known the cancer was linked to chemical hair relaxers — the "discovery rule." For hair relaxer claims, the discovery rule is particularly important because the link between hair relaxers and uterine cancer was not widely understood until the October 2022 NIH Sister Study.

Practical implications for Texas claimants:

- **Women diagnosed in October 2022 or later** are generally within the standard two-year window
- **Women diagnosed before October 2022** typically rely on the discovery rule, with the clock starting when they reasonably became aware of the connection (often through news reports, attorney communications, or healthcare provider mention)
- **Older diagnoses (pre-2020)** may still qualify under the discovery rule but face higher burden of proof
- **Wrongful death claims** for deceased women follow the same diagnosis-or-discovery rule analysis

The most important practical advice: do not assume your claim is time-barred without speaking with an attorney. Texas's discovery rule for product liability claims involving previously unknown health risks is generally favorable to plaintiffs, but the analysis is fact-specific.

## Who Qualifies to File from Texas

You may qualify to file a hair relaxer cancer lawsuit if all of the following apply to you or a deceased family member:

The defendants are the chemical manufacturers — large multinational corporations who designed, marketed, and sold these products knowing about endocrine-disrupting chemicals in their formulations.

You do not need to have saved packaging. You do not need to remember every brand. You do not need to have used only one brand.

There is no out-of-pocket cost. Hair relaxer cases are handled on contingency: no fee unless your case recovers compensation, at which point your attorney is paid from the settlement.

Filing does not affect your health insurance, Medicaid, Medicare, or any government benefits. Settlement amounts may need to satisfy medical liens, but this is normal claim resolution and your attorney handles it.
