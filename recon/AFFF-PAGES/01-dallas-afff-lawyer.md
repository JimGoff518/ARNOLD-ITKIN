## How AFFF Became a National Public Health Crisis for Firefighters

Aqueous Film Forming Foam was developed in the early 1960s by the U.S. Navy in partnership with 3M, originally to extinguish jet fuel and petroleum fires aboard aircraft carriers where water alone was useless. The chemistry that made AFFF effective — long-chain fluorinated compounds that smother oxygen and form a vapor-suppressing film — was the same chemistry that would make it one of the most persistent environmental and biological contaminants ever introduced to the modern workplace.

By the late 1960s, AFFF was being sold to civilian fire departments, airports, refineries, and chemical plants across the United States. Dallas Fire-Rescue began using AFFF in the 1970s. DFW International Airport, which opened in 1974, deployed AFFF from day one as the primary aviation firefighting agent. Every firefighter trained at DFW Airport's ARFF facility for the next four decades trained with foam containing PFAS.

The foam worked. It saved lives. It put out fires water could not touch. And every time it was deployed — every live-fire training exercise, every Class B fuel fire response, every airport emergency drill — it coated the firefighters who handled it in chemicals their bodies could not metabolize, could not eliminate, and would store in their organs, blood, and tissues for the rest of their lives.

### Why the Chemistry Made This Inevitable

PFAS are called "forever chemicals" because the carbon-fluorine bond is one of the strongest in organic chemistry. Once these compounds enter the human body — through skin absorption, inhalation, or ingestion — they bioaccumulate. They build up over years of exposure. They are passed to children through pregnancy and breastfeeding. They have been detected in the blood of virtually every American tested in national health surveys, but the concentrations found in firefighter populations are dramatically higher than in the general public.

Multiple peer-reviewed studies have documented that firefighters with significant AFFF exposure show PFAS blood serum levels three to ten times higher than non-firefighter populations. The same studies link those elevated levels to specific cancers and disorders now formally recognized in the federal litigation.

### What the Manufacturers Knew

Internal 3M documents, surfaced through litigation discovery, show that company scientists understood by the 1970s that PFOS and PFOA — the two primary PFAS compounds in early AFFF formulations — were toxic, bioaccumulative, and present in human blood at levels capable of causing harm. Internal DuPont documents similarly show decades of knowledge about PFAS health risks that the company never disclosed to firefighters, fire departments, or the public.

The manufacturers continued selling AFFF for decades after this knowledge was internally documented. They never warned firefighters. They never warned fire departments. They never updated their material safety data sheets to reflect what they knew. The federal litigation now centralized as MDL 2873 in the District of South Carolina exists because those decades of silence caused decades of cancer.

## The Federal AFFF Litigation: Where Things Stand Now

Every federal AFFF lawsuit in the United States has been consolidated into MDL 2873, presiding before Judge Richard M. Gergel in the District of South Carolina. As of April 2026, **15,222 individual claims** were pending in the MDL — a number that has roughly doubled in the last twelve months as more firefighters connect their cancer diagnoses to their AFFF exposure history.

The MDL was created to streamline pretrial proceedings — discovery, expert designations, manufacturer document production, bellwether trials — so that thousands of individual cases don't have to repeat the same procedural battles. A bellwether trial scheduled for October 2025 was taken off the calendar so the court could review the surge in recently filed claims and confirm each meets the medical and exposure proof requirements. New bellwether dates are expected as the court completes that review.

Settlement projections from legal analysts who specialize in mass torts currently range from **$20,000 to $600,000 per qualifying claim**, with the wide range reflecting differences in cancer type, exposure duration, age at diagnosis, and the specific manufacturer defendants involved. No definitive personal injury settlements have been finalized yet — those will follow the bellwether trial outcomes.

### Who Is Liable

Liability theories in the MDL include design defect, failure to warn, negligent manufacture, fraudulent concealment of known health risks, and conspiracy. Multiple manufacturers have already begun settling water-contamination cases brought by municipalities — 3M alone announced a multibillion-dollar settlement with public water suppliers in 2023 — but personal injury claims by individual firefighters remain pending.

## Why It Took Decades for This Crisis to Surface

The same chemistry that made PFAS persist in the environment also made the health consequences slow to appear. Cancers caused by PFAS exposure often manifest twenty, thirty, or even forty years after the initial exposure. A firefighter who began responding to fuel fires in 1975 might not be diagnosed with kidney cancer until 2015 — and even then, the connection to AFFF was not widely understood by treating oncologists until the late 2010s.

For decades, firefighters with elevated cancer rates were told it was an occupational hazard of the job — exposure to smoke, particulate matter, and combustion byproducts. That story was partially true. But it obscured the larger story: the very foam they used to extinguish fires was, itself, a primary cancer-causing exposure that the manufacturers knew was dangerous and concealed.

The C8 Science Panel — a multi-year independent scientific review created as part of an earlier DuPont/PFOA settlement — published its formal findings in 2012, establishing "probable links" between PFAS exposure and multiple cancers and disorders. That report became the scientific foundation for what is now MDL 2873. But it took another decade for awareness to spread through firefighter communities, for cancer registries to be cross-referenced with exposure history, and for the legal infrastructure to develop that could handle thousands of individual claims.

If you are a Dallas firefighter reading this in 2026, you are not late. You are early enough to file before the bellwether trials shape settlement amounts. Most firefighter cancer claims still have years before they reach the statute of limitations, which varies depending on when the diagnosis occurred and when the firefighter became aware of the connection between AFFF and the disease.

- Medical records documenting your qualifying diagnosis

If documentation is incomplete or unavailable, do not delay. Your attorney has tools — subpoena power, expert investigation, military records requests, FOIA — to recover documentation you cannot obtain on your own. The most damaging thing you can do is wait until your statute of limitations is closer to expiration.
