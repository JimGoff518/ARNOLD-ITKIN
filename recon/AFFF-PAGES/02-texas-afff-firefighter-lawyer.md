## Texas's Unique AFFF Exposure Profile

The federal AFFF litigation has revealed that firefighter exposure to PFAS varies dramatically based on three factors: the duration of service, the intensity of foam handling, and the frequency of live-fire training. Texas firefighters — particularly military firefighters — score high on all three dimensions for reasons specific to the state's role in U.S. military aviation and petrochemical operations.

### Texas Military Installations With Documented Heavy AFFF Use

The Department of Defense has been the largest single user of AFFF in the United States since the 1960s. Texas hosts more major military aviation installations than any other state, which means Texas military firefighters were exposed to more frequent and intensive AFFF training than firefighters in most other states. The installations with the most extensive documented AFFF use include:

- **Joint Base San Antonio (JBSA-Lackland, JBSA-Randolph, JBSA-Fort Sam Houston)** — the largest joint base in the Department of Defense, with extensive aviation firefighting training facilities. Lackland AFB hosts the Air Force's primary firefighter training school, meaning generations of military firefighters across all services trained with AFFF at Lackland before deploying worldwide.
- **Sheppard Air Force Base (Wichita Falls)** — major Air Force training installation with aviation firefighting facilities
- **Fort Hood / Fort Cavazos (Killeen)** — one of the largest active-duty armored posts in the world; significant Class B fuel fire suppression infrastructure
- **Naval Air Station Kingsville** — primary Navy jet pilot training facility; extensive aviation firefighting operations
- **Naval Air Station Corpus Christi** — Navy training and operational air station with ARFF crews
- **Dyess Air Force Base (Abilene)** — B-1B bomber base; significant aviation firefighting infrastructure
- **Goodfellow Air Force Base (San Angelo)** — firefighter training school for the Air Force, Army, Navy, and Marine Corps; this is one of the highest-exposure installations in the entire DoD system because military firefighters from all services trained with AFFF here for decades

For service members who trained at Goodfellow or Lackland and then served at additional installations during their careers, the cumulative AFFF exposure across an entire military firefighting career often dwarfs the exposure of even long-serving civilian firefighters. This cumulative exposure profile is one reason military firefighter AFFF claims often involve some of the highest documented PFAS blood serum levels in the entire MDL.

### Texas Civilian Firefighter Exposure

Beyond the military, Texas civilian firefighters across the state's largest metropolitan areas accumulated significant AFFF exposure throughout their careers. Houston Fire Department, Dallas Fire-Rescue, San Antonio Fire Department, Austin Fire Department, Fort Worth Fire Department, El Paso Fire Department, and dozens of smaller municipal departments deployed AFFF for every Class B fuel fire — vehicle fires, industrial fires, refinery emergencies, fuel spills, and aviation incidents.

Texas's petrochemical industry concentration, particularly along the Houston Ship Channel, the Beaumont-Port Arthur refinery cluster, and the Corpus Christi industrial corridor, meant Texas municipal firefighters responded to fuel fires more frequently than firefighters in most non-petrochemical states. Each response involved AFFF deployment, and each deployment involved direct foam contact for the crews involved.

## What AFFF Exposure Has Done to Texas Firefighters' Bodies

The federal AFFF MDL formally recognizes six qualifying medical conditions linked to PFAS exposure:

- **Kidney cancer** — the most common qualifying condition
- **Testicular cancer** — particularly significant in younger firefighters
- **Thyroid cancer** — papillary thyroid cancer most frequently linked
- **Liver cancer** — including hepatocellular carcinoma
- **Thyroid disease and hypothyroidism**
- **Ulcerative colitis**

Texas military firefighters present a unique medical claim profile because Department of Defense health monitoring records, military medical records, and VA health system records often provide unusually complete documentation of both exposure history and medical diagnosis chronology. This documentation matters enormously for AFFF claims. Service members and veterans who can produce DD-214 forms showing firefighter MOS or AFSC codes, training records from Lackland or Goodfellow, and VA medical records documenting qualifying conditions often have stronger documentation than many civilian claimants.

## The Federal AFFF Litigation: Current Status (April 2026)

All federal AFFF lawsuits have been consolidated into MDL 2873 before Judge Richard M. Gergel in the District of South Carolina. **15,222 individual claims** were pending as of April 2026, with the case count having approximately doubled in the prior twelve months as more firefighters — particularly military and veteran firefighters — connected their cancer diagnoses to documented AFFF exposure history.

A bellwether trial scheduled for October 2025 was taken off the calendar so the court could review the surge in recent filings and verify each case meets the medical and exposure proof requirements. New bellwether dates are expected as that review completes.

Legal analysts project settlement values between **$20,000 and $600,000 per qualifying claim**. Military firefighter claims with strong DoD documentation and high cumulative exposure history sit toward the higher end of this projected range.

### Primary Manufacturer Defendants

The principal defendants in Texas firefighter AFFF claims will most commonly include:

- **3M Company** — original AFFF developer in partnership with the U.S. Navy; supplied the majority of DoD foam contracts for decades
- **E.I. du Pont de Nemours / The Chemours Company** — long-time PFAS chemical manufacturers
- **Tyco Fire Products** — manufacturer of multiple AFFF brand formulations including Ansul brand foams used by DoD
- **Buckeye Fire Equipment** — significant supplier to municipal fire departments
- **Kidde-Fenwal** — industrial fire suppression supplier
- **National Foam (Angus Fire)** — major AFFF brand used by airports and military installations

The "fluorochemical conspiracy" theories in the MDL allege these manufacturers knew about PFAS bioaccumulation and carcinogenicity by the 1970s but coordinated to conceal those findings from federal regulators, fire departments, and the military for decades.

## Who Qualifies to File from Texas

You may qualify to file an AFFF personal injury claim if all three apply to you or a deceased family member:

1. **Documented exposure** — service as a military firefighter at any Texas installation, civilian firefighter at any Texas fire department, ARFF crew at any Texas airport, refinery firefighter, fire instructor, or fire equipment technician with documented AFFF use in your work history.
2. **Qualifying medical diagnosis** — one of the six conditions listed above, formally diagnosed and documented in medical records (including VA medical records for veterans).
3. **Medical nexus opinion** — a treating or evaluating physician's documented opinion linking your specific diagnosis to your specific PFAS exposure history.

Family members of deceased firefighters whose deaths resulted from one of the qualifying conditions may file wrongful death and survival action claims on behalf of their loved one.

## Why Texas Firefighters Should Not Wait

Texas firefighters have an unusually narrow window to file optimally because of how the federal MDL is structured. Three reasons matter:

**First, settlement structures are being negotiated now.** As bellwether trials proceed and global settlement frameworks emerge over the next 18-36 months, the qualifying criteria and documentation requirements may tighten. Claims filed earlier in the MDL process typically benefit from more flexible documentation standards than claims filed after major settlement structures are in place.

**Second, statute of limitations matters.** Texas's two-year personal injury statute of limitations runs from the date of diagnosis OR the date the firefighter knew or should have known the cancer was linked to AFFF (the "discovery rule"). The discovery rule is fact-specific and contested. The safer path is to file as soon as the diagnosis is documented rather than rely on discovery-rule extensions.

**Third, witness and documentation evidence degrades.** Military service records, training records, and supervising officer testimony all become harder to obtain as years pass. Some Texas military firefighter records from the 1970s and 1980s are already difficult to recover; cases from the 1990s onward typically still have full documentation available, but every year that passes makes the documentation harder.

## What You Need to Know If You Are Considering a Claim

Filing an AFFF personal injury claim does not require you to sue the U.S. military, the State of Texas, your fire department, or any city or county. The defendants are the chemical manufacturers — private companies that designed, marketed, and sold AFFF knowing it was harmful. Your service to your unit, to your fire department, or to the state of Texas is not on trial. The manufacturers' decision to conceal what they knew is.

For military firefighters and veterans specifically, filing an AFFF claim against the manufacturers does not affect your VA disability rating, your VA healthcare eligibility, your military pension, or any other benefit you receive from the federal government. Those benefits derive from your service; this claim derives from manufacturer misconduct.

For civilian firefighters, filing does not affect your municipal pension, workers' compensation, or fire department employment benefits.

There is no out-of-pocket cost. AFFF claims are handled on contingency: no fee unless your case recovers compensation, at which point your attorney is paid from the settlement.
