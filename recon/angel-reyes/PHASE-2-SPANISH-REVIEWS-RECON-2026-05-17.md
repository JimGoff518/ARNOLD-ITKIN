## 1. Executive summary — 7 bullets

1. **The Reyes Spanish moat is structural, not cosmetic.** Reyes runs a **full parallel `/es/` site** with Spanish URL slugs (`/es/accidentes-de-camiones/`, `/es/calculadora-de-casos/`, `/es/glosario-de-derecho/`), Spanish nav, Spanish blog, Spanish FAQ, Spanish "About," Spanish location pages, Spanish testimonials. Not auto-translated. This is a **second website's worth of content** running parallel to English.

2. **Founder origin is the unfakeable asset.** Angel Reyes is Puerto Rican (born and raised), grew up code-switching ("Moving between two worlds—two cultures, two languages"). Per his Esquire Bank profile, **30% of his clients speak ONLY Spanish.** He explicitly says they are "voiceless... they literally wouldn't have a voice without firms like ours." Goff cannot replicate the biography. Goff CAN replicate the positioning.

3. **The Univision decision built the entire firm.** Per the founder's own framing, the firm was launched by investing "most of his lawyer's fees into advertising his legal services on Univision... the single decision that led him to founding his personal injury law firm." That was 1993. Compounding TV brand for 33 years = the unkillable moat. Goff cannot out-spend Univision; Goff CAN own a smaller Spanish-language niche (mass torts in Spanish) where Reyes is absent.

4. **Review count: 11,219 verified on Birdeye / 12,000+ self-claimed on testimonials page / "15K+" in marketing.** The 15K figure includes review aggregation across platforms. Real Google-only is likely closer to 11–12K. Even at 11K, that is **~26× Mullen (430), ~6× Witherite (1,707), ~61× McCraw (182), and ~262× Goff (42)**. The gap is not closable in any 1–3 year horizon.

5. **Reviews are aggressively English-dominant despite "30% Spanish-only" client base.** Every review I sampled (15+) was English. None Spanish. This is a **collection-process tell**: review-request SMS/forms are likely English-default, even for Spanish-intake clients. **Goff opportunity:** capture a **disproportionate Spanish review share** — bilingual review-request flow makes Goff the visible Spanish-rated firm in the map pack.

6. **The site uses *informal* "tú" on practice pages but *formal* "usted" on the calculator/conversion tools.** That register-switching shows real bilingual editorial discipline. Auto-translation wouldn't do that. Goff's Spanish pages MUST do the same: warm "tú" on content, respectful "usted" on the conversion moment.

7. **The synthesized review themes ARE the playbook leak.** Reyes's testimonials page publishes the AI-summarized themes with counts: **Professional service (2.1K mentions), Easy communication (1.8K), Great results (1.5K), Caring team (1.2K), Fast response (986).** This is literally the script clients should be coached to use. Goff's review-request flow should bake these five themes into the suggested-language template.

- **Spanish location pages** for Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth (at least)

### 2b. Content samples (verbatim)

**ES car-accident H1:** "Abogados de Accidentes de Auto en Texas"

The Spanish H1 is **more keyword-rich** than the English (EN is a brand statement; ES is an SEO query match). Different writers, different briefs.

### 2c. Tone register
- **Practice pages: informal "tú"** — "te recuperas," "tu caso," "Haz clic," "Contáctanos"
- **Calculator / conversion tools: formal "usted"** — "Obtenga el dinero que usted merece"

### 2d. Code-switching audit

- settlement → acuerdos
- lawsuit → demanda

This is a **quality signal**. Many bilingual law sites bleed English legal terms; Reyes does not. Suggests **native-Spanish copywriter**, not Google Translate.

### 2e. Cultural anchors verbatim

- "comunidad hispana / hispanohablantes"
- "Luchando por Texanos"
- "se sienta escuchado" (feel heard)
- "lower socioeconomic strata... voiceless" (Angel's own framing)
- Mexican flag (not Puerto Rican, not pan-Latin) as language-toggle icon — geographic targeting of dominant DFW Hispanic demographic
